\title{Algebraic and topological invariant fields for cryptic-pocket detection\\

\author{Shane \and Xuening Wu}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We evaluate deterministic, training-free geometric detectors on the task of
recovering cryptic binding-site residues from apo receptors, using the official
CryptoBench test fold (MMseqs2 clustering at 10\% sequence identity,
cluster-disjoint). The detectors have no trained weights and no fitted
hyperparameters, and were never exposed to any CryptoBench partition; the input
contract is receptor coordinates only, enforced at runtime by a ligand-leak
guard that rejects any non-solvent heteroatom. On \NUnits{} single-chain
evaluation units a rigid geometric detector with no training of any kind attains
a per-residue ROC-AUC of \GeoAuc{} (95\% CI \GeoAucCI{}), while a locally
executed P2Rank 2.5.1 baseline, scored on its own residue-level output, reaches
\PtwoRAuc{} \PtwoRAucCI{}.

Between the two we place a detector whose entire inference path is table lookups
and integer addition. \NTabWires{} wires --- \(43\) local quantities of the apo
receptor, each read under five neighbourhood statistics --- are banded into
quaternary digits against each chain's own order statistics, and
\NTabTables{} tables of \TabWidth{} digits each hold two integers per address,
counted on the cluster-disjoint CryptoBench training fold: how many residues
landed in the cell and how many of those were cryptic. The score is the sum of
those cell ratios with integer fan-out in \([-\TabCap{}, \TabCap{}]\), followed
by one neighbourhood mean. This field attains ROC-AUC \TabAuc{} \TabAucCI{},
PR-AUC \TabPr{} \TabPrCI{}, MCC \TabMcc{} \TabMccCI{} and F1 \TabFOne{}
\TabFOneCI{}, ahead of P2Rank on all four when each detector is scored at the
operating point it ships with. None of the four differences survives holding the
threshold in common. F1 is the one this fold resolves under the shipped
conventions, by \TabFOneD{} \TabFOneDCI{} at \(p=\TabFOneP{}\), but our call is
the top \MatchOurQ{} of each chain while P2Rank's is its own pocket assignment;
giving P2Rank the same rule, at the \(q\) an identical grid search selects for it
on the training fold, leaves \MatchBDelta{} \MatchBCI{} and we withdraw the
claim.

The primary endpoint is the mean paired per-residue ROC-AUC, which is the summary
this comparison was reported under from its first reading, and it does not
resolve: \TabAucD{} \TabAucDCI{}. Neither does PR-AUC (\TabPrD{} \TabPrDCI{}),
and MCC's exclusion of zero is a property of the resampling seed rather than of
the fold. \EndNExploratoryResolving{} of \EndNExploratory{} robust summaries of
the same paired differences do exclude zero --- a \(20\%\) trimmed mean at
\PreRegTestDelta{} \PreRegTestCI{}, chosen on the training partition and
committed before the read that used it --- and we report them as exploratory
rather than confirmatory. The reason is in our own ledger: \EndReadsBefore{}
indexed readings of the held-out fold precede that commit, \EndLineageBefore{} of
them by this architecture's lineage, the last at \EndPriorBestAuc{}. Fixing the
statistic in advance rules out choosing a functional to fit the answer; it does
not rule out the fold having shaped the architecture the functional is applied
to. The distribution behind the disagreement is reported rather than summarised
away: the field is ahead on \EndWins{} of \NUnits{} chains and behind on
\EndLosses{}, with a median difference of \EndMedianDelta{}, while its worst
losses average \EndWorstLosses{} against best wins of \EndBestWins{}. It wins
more often and loses harder, which is the whole of why a trimmed mean and a mean
disagree.

Comparing against P2Rank alone would answer an easier question than a
cryptic-pocket paper poses, so both of the benchmark's own baselines were rebuilt
and run on these units. Against PocketMiner, which CryptoBench treats as the
representative cryptic-site method, the field is ahead by \PmAucOurs{}
\PmAucCIOurs{}; that margin is the least informative number here, because P2Rank
is ahead of PocketMiner too (\PmAucPtwoR{} \PmAucCIPtwoR{}), which makes the gap
evidence about transfer between two label definitions rather than a ranking of
methods. Against pLM-NN --- CryptoBench's own supervised baseline, an ESM2-3B
embedding under a trained network, reproduced here to a mean cosine of
\PlmFidelity{} against the authors' published example --- the deployed counting
field (\texttt{table\_field}) is \emph{behind} by \PlmAucDelta{} \PlmAucCI{},
losing on \PlmLoss{} of \NUnits{} chains. That deficit is not a threshold
convention either: it holds at every one of \CvN{} calling fractions, with
intervals excluding zero throughout, and the F1 and MCC deficits
(\PlmBudgetFDelta{} and \PlmBudgetMDelta{} at a common budget) survive a
Bonferroni correction that the ROC-AUC difference does not. A wider geometry
stack reaches parity with the same baseline on this fold in a development probe
that postdates the conference submission and is set out in the supplement rather
than claimed here. Scoring \GrandNOurs{} architectures of ours and all three
baselines in one pass on one
residue universe (Section~\ref{sec:grand}), the best of ours --- \GrandBest{},
an unweighted sum of within-chain standardised geometry and nonlocal-seam
scores, with no coefficient fitted --- reads \GrandBestRoc{} against pLM-NN's
\GrandPlmRoc{}, a paired difference of \GrandDPlmRoc{} \GrandDPlmRocCI{}.
\emph{The second metric reverses the sign}: on PR-AUC it is \GrandBestPr{}
against \GrandPlmPr{}, a difference of \GrandDPlmPr{} \GrandDPlmPrCI{}. Both
intervals contain zero, so this is parity in both directions rather than a lead
in either, and reporting only the ROC row would have turned a null into a win.
Against P2Rank and PocketMiner that same architecture resolves ahead on both
metrics (ROC-AUC \GrandDPTwoRoc{} \GrandDPTwoRocCI{} and \GrandDPmRoc{}
\GrandDPmRocCI{}). So the paper's confirmatory
accuracy claim remains bounded by P2Rank for the counting field that ships; the
geometry stack's official-fold parity with pLM-NN is reported as a development
probe that names its detector, not as a second confirmatory result.

Every number to this point comes from a fold this project has touched
\NFoldAccesses{} times --- \NIndexedReads{} of those carrying an explicit read
index, the rest drawing a fresh inference from per-unit numbers an earlier read
had frozen --- while \NArchOnFold{} of its architectures were scored on it, so
none of them can settle anything, and the remedy is a set that has never been
read. That accounting is itself generated from the artifacts, and an earlier
version of this sentence said three, because one macro was carrying the read
index of a single probe and being read as the programme total. From
depositions released after CryptoBench's newest structure (\ExtCutoff{}) we
assembled \ExtN{} apo--holo units carrying \ExtNPositives{} cryptic residues,
one unit per UniRef50 cluster and no cluster shared with either CryptoBench
fold, labelled by a rule recovered from the benchmark's own training records.
The set was frozen and hashed; a plan naming three co-primary comparisons, the
statistic, and the sentence to be written under each outcome was committed; and
only then was anything scored. What checks that order is the commit graph rather
than our word for it. Against P2Rank the field leads by \ExtAucPtwoR{}
\ExtAucCIPtwoR{} in mean paired per-unit ROC-AUC, ahead on \ExtWinPtwoR{} of
\ExtN{} chains, where the same comparison on the official fold gave
\ExtOldPtwoR{} and crossed zero. This is the one confirmatory accuracy result in
the paper, and because it is a ranking statistic it cannot be an artifact of the
two methods' differing calling conventions, which is the standing objection to
the F1 margin above. The deficit against pLM-NN replicates rather than
disappearing --- \ExtAucPlm{} \ExtAucCIPlm{} externally against \ExtOldPlm{}
internally, the external estimate landing inside the interval the internal read
predicted --- so external data leaves the ordering that matters most unchanged.

Three features of that result cut against us and were fixed in the plan before
it could be known which way they would cut. At a matched top-\ExtQPct{}\% budget
the F1 advantage over P2Rank fails to resolve externally as well
(\ExtBudgetFDelta{} \ExtBudgetFCI{}), so the thresholded claim is no better
supported outside the benchmark than inside it. P2Rank predicts no pocket
whatever on \ExtAbstain{} of the \ExtN{} units, which leaves its precision and
its MCC undefined there and so removes its own worst case from two of its own
averages. And the set is easier than CryptoBench in three declared respects, the
largest being a guard band that leaves pairs whose pocket moves between
\(1.5\) and \(2.5\)~\AA{} unlabelled --- exactly the borderline cases. At
\ExtN{} units it is also small: after the Bonferroni correction over the three
co-primary comparisons the P2Rank advantage survives (\ExtAucBonfPtwoR{}) and
the pLM-NN deficit does not (\ExtAucBonfPlm{}), which is a statement about power
and not about parity.

Against P2Rank, then, the fold that shaped this method shows parity and a set
that could not have shaped it shows an advantage; we let the second carry the
claim, because it is the only one whose ordering can be checked. Against the
benchmark's strongest baseline it is a deficit on both. What
the construction offers beyond that is that its every score decomposes exactly
into the table terms that produced it, and that the whole detector is
\FairModelOurs{}~MB of integers with no floating-point model evaluated at
inference. It is not faster than P2Rank in a served deployment, and
Section~\ref{sec:cost} withdraws the speed factor an earlier version of this
paper claimed. On the same wires it is also not separable from a logistic
regression (\RdLogitDelta{} \RdLogitCI{}), so the accuracy such as it is belongs
to the invariants and not to the table machinery.

Even the sign of that unresolved mean deserves less credence than an unresolved
interval usually implies, and saying why is part of the same accounting. Over
this fold the standard error of
a mean ROC-AUC is \FoldSE{}, so \TabAucD{} is \LeadInSE{} of one standard error,
drawn from a search over \NArchOnFold{} architectures of ours that have been
scored on these same units --- a count taken from an artifact ledger rather than
from memory, after a hand-written version of this disclosure proved wrong. A
maximum over that many correlated estimates is biased upward by about the size
of the margin, so we treat the ROC-AUC ordering as carrying no information and
ask the reader to do the same. Until this revision the sentence said that
\TabAucD{} \emph{was} that maximum. It was when it was written and it is no
longer: in the single-pass read of Section~\ref{sec:grand}, \GrandNAboveTab{} of
the \GrandNOurs{} architectures scored there sit above \texttt{table\_field},
the highest at \GrandBestRoc{} against its \GrandTabRoc{}. The correction cuts
against us twice over. It widens the selection surface the discount is meant to
cover rather than narrowing it, and it says plainly that the architecture this
paper ships is not the most accurate one the project has built --- it is the one
whose every score decomposes into the integer table terms that produced it,
which is a different property and the one being argued for.

MCC is the one that needs saying out loud, because two defensible choices decide
it and only one of them is honest. Each paired difference is taken on the
structures where both methods have a value, which for MCC is \TabMccNPaired{} of
\NUnits{}, since MCC is undefined wherever a confusion matrix is degenerate and
P2Rank has six such structures here while the table field has none; differencing
each method's mean over its own coverage instead would fold ``which structures
were scorable'' into a number read as ``which method is better''. On the shared
set the two MCCs are \TabMccMatched{} and \TabMccMatchedBase{}, and at our
pre-registered resampling seed that difference excludes zero. It does not
survive the seed: across \NSeedProbes{} seeds the MCC interval excludes zero in
\TabMccSeedSig{} of them, so the exclusion is a property of the draw and not of
the fold. F1 excludes zero in \TabFOneSeedSig{} of \NSeedProbes{}, and ROC-AUC
and PR-AUC in none. MCC is therefore reported as unresolved. F1's robustness to
the seed did not save it from a different objection: it is robust as a statistic
and fragile as a convention, which is what Section~\ref{sec:matched} measures.

Two design facts carry that result and both are quantitative. First, cell
occupancy: a width-six quaternary table has \(4^{6}\) cells and holds about fifty
training residues per cell, so its fraction carries a relative standard error
near 60\%, while a width-two table has sixteen cells and about
\TabCellOccupancy{} residues in each. Second, coverage: \TabWidth{}-digit tables
see only pairs, so a single partition of the wires reads a vanishing fraction of
the pair lattice, and \TabRounds{} independent partitions are needed before the
bank is competitive. A previous version of this manuscript concluded from a
single-partition experiment that the interactions available to a quaternary code
do not pay for their variance; that conclusion was an artifact of coverage and is
withdrawn. With sufficient coverage and a ridge of \TabRidge{} on the scatter of
the table outputs, the counting field is ahead of a first-order functional of the
same wires on all four metrics rather than behind it.

Selection discipline is reported in full rather than summarised. Wire families,
table width, pool size, regulariser, fan-out cap, gate radius and gate weight
were all ranked on a held-out half of the training fold sharing no UniProt
accession, where the frozen configuration measured \TabPickAuc{}. That the
choice does not depend on which half was drawn is checked two ways, neither of
them on the test fold: the architecture ranks first on all \NSplitsCv{} of
CryptoBench's own training folds, which are cluster-disjoint under the
benchmark's MMseqs2 clustering, and on all \NSplitsRh{} redraws of the
accession-disjoint half-split. The margin it leads by is a few thousandths
(\MeanMarginCv{} over the folds), so we report the ordering as stable and not
the lead as large. The architecture that ships was scored on the test fold
\NTestReads{} times while it was being built, at \ReadAucI{}, \ReadAucII{} and
the value above, with paired differences against P2Rank of \ReadDeltaI{},
\ReadDeltaII{} and \TabAucD{}; each change between readings was diagnosed and
made on the training fold, and all three readings are reported so that the
reader can see how much of the final figure is architecture and how much is
repetition. That three is a count of this lineage's development readings and
not of the programme: \NIndexedReads{} indexed reads and \NFoldAccesses{} total
accesses stand behind the paper, and \NLineageReads{} of the indexed ones name
this lineage. Three
deformation-based ablations (isotropic breathing, anisotropic ANM shear,
ultrametric-gated shear) score at or below the rigid detector on every metric and
are reported as falsified rather than omitted. All numbers are regenerated by a
fail-closed pipeline with per-file SHA-256 verification and explicit
denominators. We claim no binding affinity and no clinical or design utility
(\texttt{clinical\_grade=false}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope}
This paper makes three claims about the official CryptoBench receptor-only test
fold. First, a detector whose inference is table lookups and integer addition
over quaternary digits of receptor geometry is ahead of a locally executed P2Rank
on all four official metrics at each detector's shipped operating point, and one
paired difference is resolved: the ROC-AUC difference under a summary statistic
fixed on the training partition before the fold was read. The F1 difference,
which resolves under the shipped conventions, does not survive binarising both
methods by a common rule, and is reported as convention-dependent rather than as
an advantage (Section~\ref{sec:matched}). Second, the quantity that governs such a detector
is not the width of its tables but the occupancy of their cells and the coverage
of the digit pairs: narrow tables read many times beat wide tables read once, by
a margin this fold does resolve. Third, restricting the resolution path to
counting costs nothing relative to a first-order functional of the same wires,
which is the opposite of what we reported previously and is explained in
Section~\ref{sec:results}.

Explicitly not claimed: a resolved advantage over P2Rank on PR-AUC or MCC, on
ROC-AUC in the mean, or on F1 under any matched threshold; parity on any other
benchmark; binding affinity; candidate efficacy; drug
design utility; clinical readiness; or global novelty. The field's cells and its
integer fan-out are compiled on the CryptoBench training fold, so it is
\emph{not} training-free; the training-free detectors are reported separately and
are weaker. The fan-out comes from one regularised closed-form solve, which is a
real-valued object at compile time even though inference is integral, and the
paper says so wherever the field is described. Appendix~\ref{app:gf4} is an
algebraic negative control that is excluded from these claims and from every
generalization statement.

\section{Data}
The benchmark is the official CryptoBench test fold (Skrhak et al.,
\emph{Bioinformatics} 41(1):btae745, 2025; dataset \url{https://osf.io/pz4a9/}),
whose splits are cluster-disjoint under MMseqs2 at 10\% sequence identity.
Receptors are materialized from RCSB and every receptor and label file is pinned
by SHA-256 in a manifest; the loader refuses to run if the manifest is absent,
if the declared clustering method or identity threshold differs, if any hash
mismatches, or if a cluster identifier appears in more than one split. There is
no fallback path: a smaller pilot subset cannot be substituted for this fold.

The evaluation unit is a \((\text{pdb},\text{chain})\) pair, not a PDB entry:
192 units span 190 distinct entries because two entries contribute two chains
each. A further 38 fold units are multi-chain assemblies and are excluded before
evaluation, enumerated in the manifest, because the residue indexing used here is
chain-agnostic and cannot address such assemblies unambiguously. The exclusion is
recorded rather than silent.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
\paragraph{Input contract.} Every detector receives receptor coordinates only.
A decorator asserts, at call time, that the file carries no non-solvent HETATM
record and no non-polymer ATOM record; a violation is recorded as a crash, not
skipped. An import-graph test additionally forbids any detector module from
importing the scorer, so a label cannot reach a predictor through code.

\paragraph{Rigid geometric detector.} Free grid points outside the van der Waals
surface are scored by a buriedness functional, the fraction of probe directions
blocked by an atom within a ray cylinder. Candidate centres are the most buried
points, deduplicated by a minimum separation. There are no trained weights and no
fitted hyperparameters; the geometric constants are fixed a priori and are not
tuned against any CryptoBench partition.

\paragraph{Deformation ablations.} Two ablations test whether admitting
deformation improves recovery of pockets that are closed in the apo frame. The
isotropic ablation applies a fixed set of radial dilations. The anisotropic
ablation replaces isotropic dilation with the receptor's own low-frequency,
non-rigid modes from an anisotropic network model: a candidate is promoted when a
small shear opens a large still-enclosed cavity at that site. Shear amplitudes
are fixed by the spectrum as \(c_k=\Delta x\sqrt{\lambda_1/\lambda_k}\), so no
scalar is tuned. The eigen-decomposition is pinned for determinism by a fixed
Lanczos start vector, zero convergence tolerance, and an eigenvector phase
convention; repeated runs are byte-identical.

\paragraph{Three classes of detector, and what ``training-free'' means here.}
The detectors above see no CryptoBench partition of any kind: their constants are
fixed a priori and the apo receptor is their only input. The field detectors
below are not of that class and we do not describe them as training-free. They
are compiled on the official CryptoBench training fold, which is cluster-disjoint
from the test fold at 10\% sequence identity under the same MMseqs2 partition.
Among them we draw one further distinction, and it is mechanical rather than
rhetorical. The counting fields store only pairs of integers --- how many
residues addressed a cell, and how many of them were cryptic --- and every
quantity they read at inference time is a ratio of two such integers; no gradient
step is taken, no real-valued weight is solved for, and no continuous
discriminant is fitted. The linear field relaxes exactly that last property: it
solves one regularised symmetric system for a real coefficient vector. It still
takes no gradient step, runs no iteration, differentiates nothing, and reads no
test residue, but it is a fitted model and every table in this paper labels it as
one. We separate the three classes rather than averaging over them, because
averaging over what a method was allowed to see is what makes such tables
misleading.

\paragraph{Quaternary resolution field.} Six geometric invariants per residue are
banded into quaternary digits against the residue's own chain quartiles, which
is a comparator network over the chain and carries no constant between chains.
The digit word addresses a dense table of \(4^{6}\) cells holding the two
integer counters. The addressable width is not free: with \(N\) training residues
at cryptic base rate \(r\), a cell holds \(rN/4^{d}\) expected positives, and
requiring at least one gives \(d \le \log_4(rN)\). On this fold
\(rN = 13\,524\) and the bound is \(d \le 6.87\), so a flat word may not exceed
seven digits. An earlier ten-digit variant violated it: \(4^{10}\) cells against
\(13\,524\) positives left 98.9\% of the address space undrivable.

\paragraph{Algebraic counting field.} To reach the descriptive richness of a
feature-based predictor without leaving exact quantities, the word is widened to
35 invariants, each a closed-form algebraic or integer topological function of
the receptor: solid-angle occlusion and protrusion; the Laplacian spectrum of the
induced neighbourhood graph together with the Betti numbers of the local pocket
lining; the closed-form least-squares gradient and discrete Laplacian of the
packing-density field; non-Archimedean ball geometry and the residue valuation;
inertia-tensor anisotropy, planarity and sphericity; and depth, coordination and
contact order. Because the capacity bound forbids addressing 35 digits at once,
the invariants are partitioned into six thematic blocks of at most six digits,
each block compiled into its own dense table, and the blocks fused by addition of
their cell fractions. Fusing rather than cascading is deliberate: a block table
estimates its fraction from about 57 residues, whose standard error is 53\% of
the base rate, and banding such an estimate into two bits is an irreversible
decision taken on a noise-dominated statistic. Measured on this fold, every
cascaded topology we compiled scored below the flat control, while parallel
fusion scored above it. Block multiplicities in the sum are small integers given
by the rank of each block's own compiled Gini index, i.e.\ fan-out replication of
a table, not a real coefficient. A final spatial gate adds, at five radii, the
mean of the fused score over the closed geometric neighbourhood, which states
combinationally the fact that a cryptic site is a contiguous patch of residues
rather than an isolated one.

\paragraph{Wire expansion.} The linear field widens the word from the 35
invariants to \NLinWires{} wires without introducing any learned representation.
Seven published per-residue constants are appended (Kyte--Doolittle hydropathy,
side-chain volume, aromatic ring count, formal charge at pH 7, hydrogen-bond
donor and acceptor counts, and rotatable side-chain bonds), together with one
counted propensity \(P(\text{cryptic}\mid\text{residue type})\) compiled on the
training fold alone and carried into the artifact, so no test residue ever
contributes a count to it. Each of the resulting 43 quantities is then taken both
at the residue and as its mean over the 6, 14 and 20\,\AA{} neighbourhoods, which
states at three scales the fact that a cryptic site is a property of a
neighbourhood rather than of one residue. Quantization is unchanged: each wire is
banded into a quaternary digit against its own chain's order statistics.

\paragraph{Five statistics of a neighbourhood.} The table field takes the wire
expansion one step further, and the step is the one that matters for its result.
Each of the 43 local quantities is read not only at the residue and as its
neighbourhood mean, but under five statistics of the same fixed geometric
adjacency \(N_r(i)=\{j:\lVert c_i-c_j\rVert\le r\}\):
\[
C_r[x]_i=\frac{1}{|N_r|}\!\!\sum_{j\in N_r(i)}\!\! x_j,\quad
\mathrm{sd}_r[x]_i=\sqrt{C_r[x^2]_i-C_r[x]_i^2},\quad
D_r[x]_i=x_i-C_r[x]_i,\quad
K_r[x]_i=\frac{|\{j\in N_r(i):x_j<x_i\}|}{|N_r(i)|},
\]
with the mean at \(r\in\{6,10,14,20,26\}\) and the other three at
\(r\in\{6,14,20\}\), for \(43\times 15=\NTabWires{}\) wires. None of the four is
a kernel, a bandwidth or a fitted constant; each is an unweighted statistic of a
ball. The second moment states how uniform a neighbourhood is, which the mean
cannot: two residues can share a mean hydrophobicity because every neighbour is
moderately oily or because half are oily and half are charged, and a cryptic site
sits at the interface between such regions. The centred difference is a discrete
Laplacian, large where a residue is unlike its surroundings; as a real number it
is a difference of two wires already present and adds nothing to a linear
functional, but the field does not read real numbers --- each wire is separately
ranked within its chain and cut into a quaternary digit, and the digit of a
difference is not a function of the digits of its terms. The local rank is the
only one of the four that is not a moment: it is invariant to any monotone
rescaling of the wire, and it separates a residue that is marginally the most
hydrophobic of its neighbours from one that is marginally the least, which
\(D_r\) reports as two small numbers of opposite sign and comparable magnitude.

\paragraph{The table field.} \NTabTables{} tables are formed as \TabRounds{}
independent random partitions of the \NTabWires{} wires into groups of
\TabWidth{}, so each wire appears at most once per round, each time with
different partners, and coverage of the digit-pair lattice comes from the number
of rounds rather than from the width of any table. \NTabWires{} is odd, so one
wire is left over in each round and its singleton group addresses no pair and is
dropped: \NWireFull{} wires appear in \TabRounds{} tables and \NWireShort{} in
\TabRounds{} less one. Appendix~\ref{app:compile} gives the seed, and the
generator of that appendix redraws the bank from it and refuses to emit unless
the redraw matches the shipped bank pair for pair. Each table stores
\(\mathrm{tot}_k[a]\) and \(\mathrm{pos}_k[a]\) per address, counted on the
training fold; an address no training residue reached takes the fold's base rate,
which is the only value that adds no information, and there are
\NTabCellsEmpty{} such cells out of \NTabCells{}. The score is
\[
S(i)=\sum_{k} m_k\,\frac{\mathrm{pos}_k[a_k(i)]}{\mathrm{tot}_k[a_k(i)]},
\qquad m_k\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[-\TabCap{},\TabCap{}],
\qquad
F(i)=S(i)+\TabGateWeight{}\,\bar{S}_{\TabGateRadius{}}(i)\,
\frac{\mathrm{sd}(S)}{\mathrm{sd}(\bar S_{\TabGateRadius{}})},
\]
so inference is one cell read per table, an integer-weighted sum, and one
neighbourhood mean. In the shipped field, compiled over all \NTrainUnits{}
training units (\NTabTrainRes{} residues), \NTabUsedFullFold{} tables carry a
non-zero fan-out and the total is \TabFanOut{}. The count is a property of the
fit set and not of the configuration: the same specification counted on the
cluster-disjoint selection half used for architecture choice yields
\NTabUsedFitHalf{} non-zero tables and a total of \TabFanOutFitHalf{}, with
\NTabCellsEmptyFitHalf{} never-addressed cells against \NTabCellsEmpty{} for the
full fold. Fewer cells go unaddressed and less total fan-out is spent as the fit
set grows, which is the expected direction; the two numbers are reported
separately here because a single name for both is what made them look like a
contradiction. The fan-out is obtained by rounding the solution of one symmetric
system in the table outputs, regularised by \(\TabRidge{}\) of its trace, onto the
integer grid. That is a real-valued object at compile time and we label it as
such: what is integral is the inference path, not the compilation. The gate is
matched to the standard deviation of \(S\) rather than to its maximum, because
the maximum of a score field over a chain is an order statistic of a handful of
residues and a max-normalised gate therefore mixes in a different amount on every
structure. The operating point for MCC and F1 is the per-chain top
\(\TabOperatingQ{}\) fraction, fixed on the training fold by maximising pooled
training F1 over a grid from \(0.02\) to \(0.40\). P2Rank has no such knob and is
scored at its own pocket assignment; Section~\ref{sec:matched} runs the identical
grid on P2Rank's training scores so that the two can be compared at one threshold
as well as at two.

\paragraph{Linear readout, and why table width is the free variable.} The readout
is one regularised solve, \(w=(A^{\top}A+\lambda\,\bar{\sigma}I)^{-1}A^{\top}t\),
over the standardised digits, with \(\lambda=\LinRidge{}\) and \(t\) the centred
label. The score is then \(s+\LinGateWeight{}\,\bar{s}\), where \(\bar{s}\) is
the mean of \(s\) over the closed \LinGateRadius{}\,\AA{} neighbourhood rescaled
to the spread of \(s\) --- the same patch statement the counting field makes,
applied to a continuous field.

Which readout to use is not a matter of taste here, and the quantity that decides
it is the width of the table. A width-\(d\) quaternary table has \(4^{d}\) cells,
so on \(N=\NTrainResidues{}\) training residues at base rate \TrainRate{} a cell
holds \(N/4^{d}\) residues and its fraction has relative standard error
\(\sqrt{(1-r)/(rN/4^{d})}\). At \(d=6\) that is about 60\%: the bank models the
joint distribution inside each window exactly, but each exact statement is about
a noisy quantity. At \(d=2\) it is about 3\%, and there are only \(\binom{K}{2}\)
such tables over the top \(K\) wires, few enough to combine by the same single
solve. A first-order functional sits at the other end, representing no
interaction at all but estimating each coefficient from the whole fold. We
therefore compiled all three and let the training fold choose, which is reported
in Section~\ref{sec:readout} rather than assumed here.

\paragraph{Metrics.} All four metrics are computed over the same residue universe
(every residue of the apo chain) at the same operating point (residue belongs to
any predicted pocket): ROC-AUC, average precision (PR-AUC), MCC and F1.
Uncertainty is a paired bootstrap over evaluation units, 10{,}000 resamples, 95\%
percentile CI, with the same resample indices applied to every method so that
between-method correlation is preserved. A metric that is undefined for a
structure is null and is dropped from that metric's denominator; it is never
imputed as zero. Per-unit values are keyed on \((\text{pdb},\text{chain})\), and a
colliding key raises rather than overwriting a row.

\paragraph{Baselines.} P2Rank 2.5.1 is executed locally with a pinned version and
is a scored baseline. A uniform bounding-box sampler provides a chance floor.
PocketMiner, which the benchmark's own paper treats as the representative
cryptic-site method, is also executed locally: the published artifact is a trained
model with no per-residue predictions for this fold, so the checkpoint is restored
at a pinned commit and run on the same \NUnits{} single-chain units. Before any
label on the held-out fold was opened, the rebuild was required to reproduce
PocketMiner's own published test set --- ROC-AUC \PmSelfAuc{} against the
published \PmSelfPublished{}, with its positive and negative residue counts
matching exactly --- so that whatever the comparison finds cannot be attributed to
a baseline assembled wrongly. A metric is never imputed when a tool is
unavailable, and tool unavailability is never counted as a miss.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
The official evaluation emits \NRows{} telemetry rows, \NUnits{} evaluation
units times \NMethods{} methods, with per-residue metrics available on all of
them and no silent drops. Every method returns a prediction on
\NUnits{}/\NUnits{} units.

P2Rank is scored on the residue-level table it natively emits
(\texttt{*\_residues.csv}): the calibrated \texttt{probability} column as the
continuous per-residue score, and its own \texttt{pocket > 0} assignment as the
binary operating point. An earlier revision of this harness instead reconstructed
a residue signal from pocket centres, keeping the top 5 pockets and weighting
every residue within 6\,\AA{} of a centre by \(1/\text{rank}\). That is not
P2Rank's prediction and it is lossy: \(1/\text{rank}\) is a five-valued staircase
that cannot resolve the residue ordering P2Rank emits, and truncation ties every
remaining residue at zero. Under that harness P2Rank measured ROC-AUC 0.621;
scored on its own output it reaches \PtwoRAuc{}. We report the corrected figure.

Whether that constitutes reproducing the published baseline is a question about
aggregation, and it is answered by measurement rather than assertion.
CryptoBench pools over every residue in the subset; this paper takes the mean
over structures, because a paired test needs one value per structure and because
pooling lets a single 900-residue protein outvote forty small ones. Recomputed
under the published convention -- pooled, P2Rank's own probability column, its
own pocket assignment as the operating point -- five of the seven published
quantities return within 0.03: AUC \(0.800\) against \(0.81\), AUPRC \(0.236\)
against \(0.21\), accuracy \(0.857\) against \(0.85\), FPR \(0.124\) against
\(0.14\), MCC \(0.275\) against \(0.27\). TPR is \(0.545\) against \(0.62\); the
subsets are not identical, since the published row is CB-P2RANK-apo and we
evaluate the \NUnits{} single-chain units of the 222 apo structures after
excluding 38 multi-chain assemblies with ambiguous residue numbering, and recall
is where a subset difference shows first.

The published F1 of \(0.81\) is not reproduced under any convention we can
compute: positive-class F1 is \(0.303\), the class-weighted average \(0.885\),
the macro average \(0.612\). On a task with a \(5.7\,\%\) positive rate a
class-weighted F1 is dominated by the negatives and a value near \(0.81\) is
attainable by predicting almost nothing, so we neither reproduce that column nor
compare against it. Every F1 in this paper is the positive-class F1.

\paragraph{The architecture was not chosen on the test fold.} \NCandidates{}
fusion architectures were compared during development. Selecting among them by
test-fold score and then reporting the winner's test score would make the
reported figure the maximum of \NCandidates{} noisy estimates. The choice was
therefore made on the training fold alone, split into two halves that share no
UniProt accession (\NSelectFitUnits{} units to compile on, \NSelectPickUnits{} to
rank on), and the official test fold is read once, for the single architecture
that procedure names. It names ``\SelectedArch{}'' at ROC-AUC
\SelectedArchAuc{} on the held-out training half, which is the architecture
reported here; the training-only ranking of all \NCandidates{} candidates agrees
with the test-fold ranking, so the test figure can be read as an out-of-sample
estimate rather than a selected maximum.

We are deliberate about what that half-split guarantees. It is disjoint by
accession, which is finer than the MMseqs2 10\% clustering the benchmark is
defined by: a 10\% cluster can span several accessions, so two homologues can
sit on opposite sides of it. The clustering separates train from test, because
CryptoBench's folds were built under it, but the cluster assignment is not
distributed with the data and cannot be reconstructed from it. An earlier
revision of this paragraph called the half-split cluster-disjoint, which
overstated it.

\paragraph{The architecture was not chosen by one split either.} A single
half-split leaves the winner carrying the maximum of \NCandidates{} estimates
whose top few are close, so the selection is repeated, and neither repetition
reads the test fold. The coarser test uses CryptoBench's own partition:
its training data ships as \NSplitsCv{} folds built by its authors under the
10\% clustering and pairwise disjoint in both PDB id and accession, and holding
each out in turn is a cluster-level cross-validation under the benchmark's
clustering rather than a proxy for it. The frozen architecture ranks first on
\NFirstCv{} of \NSplitsCv{}, worst rank \WorstRankCv{}, at mean ROC-AUC
\MeanAucCv{} (sd \SdAucCv{}). The finer test redraws the accession-disjoint
half-split under \NSplitsRh{} further seeds; it ranks first on \NFirstRh{} of
\NSplitsRh{}, worst rank \WorstRankRh{}, at \MeanAucRh{} (sd \SdAucRh{}).

The ordering is therefore stable, and the margin it is stable by is small. The
runner-up is ``\RunnerUpArch{}'' --- the same bank under the other fusion rule
--- at a mean \MeanMarginCv{} behind over the folds and \MeanMarginRh{} over the
seeds, never further behind than \WorstMarginCv{} and \WorstMarginRh{}
respectively. What separates the top two from the rest is the multi-scale
spatial gate, not the fusion rule: both gated variants outrank every ungated
candidate in all \NSplitsCv{} folds. We read this as evidence that the frozen
configuration is not an artefact of one draw, and not as evidence that it is
meaningfully better than its nearest alternative. Every per-split ranking behind
these counts is in
\texttt{results/architecture\_sweep/REPEATED\_TRAIN\_SELECTION.json}, and
\texttt{make crossval} rejects a summary that does not follow from them.

\paragraph{What the fold mean is made of.} A difference in a fold mean does not
say whether one method is slightly better on the same structures or very
different on different ones, and here it is the second. Taking per-residue F1
\(\ge \CaseLocateF{}\) as locating the site --- a low bar, stated rather than
tuned --- \NLocBoth{} of the \NUnits{} units are located by both methods,
\NLocOurs{} by the table field alone, \NLocPTwo{} by P2Rank alone, and
\NLocNeither{} by neither. The disagreement is therefore not a margin on shared
successes: the two methods succeed on substantially different structures, and
the modal outcome, by a factor of three over everything else, is that both fail.

Figure~\ref{fig:cases} shows one unit from each outcome, chosen by rule rather
than by eye --- the clearest joint success, the largest F1 margin each way, and
the largest labelled pocket among the units neither locates. On \CaseBoth{} both
methods place their calls on the site (F1 \CaseBothFOurs{} and \CaseBothFPTwo{},
call centroids \CaseBothOffOurs{} and \CaseBothOffPTwo{}\,\AA{} from the pocket
centre). On \CaseOurs{} the field is on the site and P2Rank is beside it
(\CaseOursFOurs{} against \CaseOursFPTwo{}, centroids \CaseOursOffOurs{} and
\CaseOursOffPTwo{}\,\AA{} out); on \CaseTheirs{} the positions are reversed
(\CaseTheirsFOurs{} against \CaseTheirsFPTwo{}, \CaseTheirsOffOurs{} and
\CaseTheirsOffPTwo{}\,\AA{}). Neither failure is a diffuse one: in both, the
losing method commits to a compact region that is simply the wrong region.

\paragraph{Both methods share the failure, and it is a burial prior.} Grouping
the fold by outcome and measuring how buried each labelled site is --- C-alpha
neighbours within 10\,\AA{}, against the same chain's mean --- the sites both
methods find sit \BurialBoth{} above their chain's mean coordination, and the
sites neither finds sit \BurialNeither{} above it. Both methods call buried
residues regardless: on the units neither locates, the table field's calls are
\BurialCallsNeitherOurs{} above the chain mean and P2Rank's
\BurialCallsNeitherPTwo{}. So this is one shared prior rather than two methods
disagreeing. It pays where the cryptic site happens to be buried and both fail
together where it is not, which is most of the fold. That is a limitation of the
class of method, not of one implementation, and no reordering of the table in
this paper addresses it. Every count here is recomputed from the committed
labels and raw per-residue output by
\texttt{tools/select\_case\_studies.py}, and \texttt{make cases} rejects a
figure or a sentence that stops following from them.

\paragraph{What limits the counting field.} Holding the estimator fixed and
widening the invariant word from six to \NAlgFeatures{} moves a closed-form
linear functional of the same invariants from ROC-AUC \FisherSix{} to \FisherAll{} on this
fold, so the descriptors carry the signal. Pushed through counter tables the
same invariants reach \AlgAuc{}. The gap is not descriptive, then, but a
property of the estimator. A capacity argument is available and the arithmetic
is sound: a table over \(d\) quaternary digits has \(4^{d}\) free cells and is
bounded by \(d \le \log_4(rN) = 6.87\) here, whereas a linear form over \(M\)
rank features is determined by \(\Theta(M^{2})\) quantities, which for
\(M=\NAlgFeatures{}\) is \(1\,225\) against \(13\,524\) available positives, so
the linear form can address all \NAlgFeatures{} dimensions at once while no
admissible table can address more than seven. An earlier version of this paper
stopped there and attributed the deficit to that bound. It does not survive
being tested: Section~\ref{sec:ai} raises the bound from seven digits to
forty-one, removes it entirely, and removes it in the other direction, and none
of the three moves the test fold. What does account for the deficit, on the
same cluster-disjoint half, is the fan-out --- the \NAlgTables{} integers that
add the block tables together --- and \GapExtraWires{} wires that the fitted
readout is handed and the counting field never sees.

That bound is about one table and it remains correct; what does not follow from it
is the conclusion we drew, that the counting construction has a ceiling below the
linear form. The bound limits how many digits one address may carry, not how many
addresses a bank may read. A bank of \NTabTables{} two-digit tables commits
\(\TabWidth{} \times \NTabTables{}\) digit slots and spends
\(\NTabCells{} - \NTabCellsEmpty{}\) occupied cells, each estimated from
\TabCellOccupancy{} residues, so it addresses all \NTabWires{} wires while no
individual address exceeds two --- and it measures above the linear form on all
four metrics (Section~\ref{sec:coverage}). The capacity theorem and the
measurement agree; our reading of the theorem did not.

\subsection{Choosing the readout on the training fold}
\label{sec:readout}
The readout was chosen by the same discipline as the fusion architecture and on
the same cluster-disjoint halves of the training fold: compile on
\NSelectFitUnits{} units, rank on \NSelectPickUnits{} units whose clusters the
first half never saw, and read the test fold once for the winner. On that
held-out training half the integer-multiplicity table bank reaches \PickCount{},
a linear functional of the \NLinWires{} first-order digits reaches
\PickLinDigits{}, and the same functional over the digit \emph{indicators} ---
which can represent a non-monotone response to a wire, at four times the
parameter count --- reaches \PickLinOnehot{}, slightly worse. A linear
combination of the bank's own cell fractions reaches only \PickLinTables{}: the
tables lose more to cell variance than they gain from modelling the joint
distribution, and no way of weighting them recovers it.

That diagnosis predicts where an interaction model should live, so we tested it
directly by varying the table width. Width-two tables over the top \(K\) wires
have sixteen cells each, estimated to about 3\% rather than 60\%, and there are
few enough of them to combine by the same solve. Their best configuration,
\PickPairBestName{}, reaches \PickPairBest{} --- above the width-six bank, below
the first-order functional, and monotonically worse as \(K\) grows. We concluded
from that sweep that pairwise interactions among quaternary-coded invariants
carry no signal beyond first order that survives their own variance.

\subsection{Withdrawing that conclusion: it was coverage, not variance}
\label{sec:coverage}
The conclusion above is wrong, and the reason it is wrong is worth more than the
conclusion was. Every width-two configuration in that sweep took the top \(K\)
wires in index order and cut them into consecutive disjoint pairs, so each wire
appeared in exactly one table and the bank read \(K/2\) of the \(\binom{K}{2}\)
pairs available. At \(K=90\) that is 45 pairs out of 4005, one percent of the
lattice, and ``monotonically worse as \(K\) grows'' is what a fixed one-percent
sample looks like when the denominator grows. The experiment varied the wrong
quantity.

Replacing the single partition with \(R\) independent random partitions of all
wires --- every wire appears at most once per round and meets different partners
each round --- makes the trend reverse. On the same held-out training half, and
with the scatter of the table outputs regularised by a ridge of \TabRidge{} of
its trace, the bank rises with \(R\) and passes the first-order functional. The
frozen configuration uses \TabRounds{} rounds over \NTabWires{} wires, giving
\NTabTables{} tables and \NTabCells{} cells of which \NTabCellsEmpty{}
(\TabCellsEmptyPct{}\%) are never addressed by any training residue, with
\TabCellOccupancy{} residues in the average occupied cell. It measures
\TabPickAuc{} on the held-out half against \PickLinDigits{} for the first-order
functional, and \TabAuc{} on the test fold.

Two further facts belong with it. The regulariser is load-bearing rather than
cosmetic: unregularised, the same solve measured 0.7844 at 1032 tables and 0.6846
at 1720, because random pairs repeat as the pool grows and the scatter goes
near-singular --- the same collinearity described in the note below, now appearing
as an instability in double precision rather than as round-off in single. And a purely
counting fusion, with multiplicities read off each table's compiled Gini index
instead of a solve, reaches only 0.6994 on this bank: with a thousand tables of
comparable individual informativeness, a rank statistic cannot tell which of them
are restating each other, and the ability to subtract is what the solve supplies.
An additive-only stagewise search, which can decline a redundant table but cannot
correct for one already taken, reaches 0.7687. We report these because they bound
how much of the result is counting and how much is the solve.

One methodological note belongs in the record. A first pass at this sweep
accumulated the \(1770\times1770\) Gram matrix in single precision and reported
that adding tables destroys the fit, collapsing from 0.74 to 0.60. Pair
fractions are strongly collinear --- two tables sharing a wire agree on a quarter
of their address --- so the Gram matrix is near-singular and single-precision
accumulation loses exactly the small eigenvalues that carry the interaction
signal. The collapse was round-off, not a property of the model class. We report
it because the failure mode is invisible in the output: the numbers are
plausible, ordered, and wrong.

\paragraph{Negative result: deformation does not help.} Both deformation
ablations score strictly below the rigid detector on all four metrics. The
anisotropic modes used here are global and low-frequency; they act as
macroscopic hinges that separate surface regions and create decoy volume, which
drags the predicted centre away from the true site. The correct next step
indicated by this failure is spatially gated, localized shear with rigid-hit
anchoring. We report this as a falsified hypothesis rather than removing the
ablations from the paper.

\section{AI experiments: a quotient bound, a gain that did not transfer, a wrong
diagnosis corrected, a functional fixed in advance, a bank widened to no effect,
and a screen that named three live families after six null ones}
\label{sec:ai}
Everything in this section --- the bound, the construction that follows from it,
the fourteen-split validation, the fourth and fifth readings of the held-out
fold, the preregistration that licensed the fifth, the two descriptor
generators of Section~\ref{sec:banks}, the six negative results, and the screen
of Section~\ref{sec:families} together with the three families it named and
the arms that control them --- was produced by an AI agent working inside this
repository under the access discipline the rest of the paper describes. It is
separated out because the provenance is different, not because the standard is:
the artifacts are the same artifacts, the gates are the same gates, and the
readings of the test fold are logged in the same ledger as the other three.

Two properties of that provenance are worth stating, because they are what makes
the section readable rather than an appeal to novelty. **The negatives are here
in the same proportion they occurred.** Six wire families were null before any
was not, the widened bank was a failure measured against the deployed detector
after being a success against its ancestor, and the capacity diagnosis in
Section~\ref{sec:results} was the agent's own and was withdrawn by the agent's
own measurement. **And every family carries an arm designed to destroy it.** The
screen that named the three live families is one sentence and would be worth
nothing if it had been fitted after the fact, so each family is measured against
a control that spends the same table budget on wires already deployed and
against a permutation that preserves every column's marginal while destroying
the correspondence between a row and its residue. A screen that predicts a sign
before the run, on a family whose two controls both come out null or negative, is
a different object from a screen that explains the runs already made.

\paragraph{The bound counts cells, and cells are a function of the symmetry.}
Section~\ref{sec:results} attributes the counting field's deficit to capacity: a
dense quaternary table over \(d\) digits has \(L^{d}\) cells, admissible only
while \(L^{d} \le rN\), so at \(L=4\) it is confined to \(d \le
\QuoDenseBound{}\). That argument counts cells that the table is free to set
independently. A table required to be invariant under a group \(G\) acting on
its digit positions has no such freedom: its free cells are the orbits of \(G\),
and it is the orbit count that \(rN\) must populate. For the symmetric group
\(S_d\) --- the table may depend on which levels occur and how often, but not on
which position carries them --- an orbit is a multiset, so
\begin{equation}
  \bigl|\Omega_{S_d}(d, L)\bigr| = \binom{d + L - 1}{d},
\end{equation}
polynomial in \(d\) where \(L^{d}\) is exponential. The admissible width at
\(L=4\) moves from \QuoDenseWidth{} to \QuoSymWidth{}: all \NAlgFeatures{}
invariants fit inside one symmetric table, at \QuoSymCellsAll{} orbits against
the \NTrainPositives{} positives available to drive them. The exchange runs the
other way as well, and that is the direction that mattered. Holding \(d = 6\), a
dense table is stuck at four levels (\QuoDenseCells{} cells) while a symmetric
one reaches twelve; at eight levels it costs \QuoSymCellsEight{} orbits against
\QuoSymCells{} at four. The quotient buys resolution per digit, which is
precisely what the tables were losing to the continuous linear readout.

\paragraph{What the symmetry costs, and which one to impose.} A symmetric table
cannot say which invariant is extreme, only how many sit at each level. Whether
that trade is worth taking is not settled by the bound, and on this fold the
widest version of it is a failure: one \(S_{35}\) table over the whole word
scores \QuoWorstArchDelta{} against the dense bank, the largest deficit of any
candidate tried. What survives is the Young subgroup \(S_6 \times \cdots \times
S_6 \times S_5\) --- exchangeable inside each thematic group of invariants,
rigid across groups --- which quotients only the ordering among quantities that
measure the same kind of thing. Widening the blocks to twelve, which the bound
permits and exchangeability does not, loses a further four hundredths.

\paragraph{On the training fold the construction wins everywhere.}
\QuoNCandidates{} candidates were compared on \QuoNSplits{} splits, none of them
the test fold: CryptoBench's own four training folds held out in turn, which are
disjoint under the benchmark's MMseqs2 10\% clustering, and ten redraws of the
accession-disjoint half-split. The winner is a bank of \QuoNTables{} quotient
tables at eight, six and four levels, fused by the same integer fan-out and read
through the same multi-scale gate as the frozen detector, and it beats the dense
bank it replaces on \QuoNBeating{} of \QuoNSplits{} splits, by a mean of
\QuoTrainGain{} with a worst case of \QuoTrainWorst{}. Nothing about the
detector was changed except the address.

\paragraph{On the held-out fold it wins nothing.} Reading
\QuoReadIndex{} of the official fold scores the frozen construction at
\QuoTestAuc{}, against the dense counting field's \AlgAuc{}: a paired difference
of \QuoDAf{}, 95\% CI \QuoDAfCI{}, \(p = \QuoDAfP{}\). The gain did not transfer
--- not reduced, absent. Against P2Rank the field remains separably behind at
\QuoDPTwo{}, CI \QuoDPTwoCI{}. Because the claim being made is a negative one,
the same code path was used to recompile and rescore the frozen dense bank,
which returns \QuoControlRepro{} against its telemetry value of \AlgAuc{};
rescoring a frozen detector is recorded in the ledger and is not a reading. So
the pipeline reproduces the number it is measured against, and the failure to
transfer is a property of the construction rather than of the harness.

\paragraph{What a fourteen-split validation was and was not worth.} The
construction was found by trying roughly thirty variants on a single
accession-disjoint half-split, where it measured \QuoOneSplitGain{}. Over the
fourteen splits it measures \QuoTrainGain{}, and the difference between those
two numbers is what selecting on one split is worth; both are recorded in the
selection artifact rather than the smaller one quietly replacing the larger.
What the cross-validation did not do is predict the test fold. A gain that holds
on four cluster-disjoint folds and ten accession-disjoint halves, without a
single exception, still vanished on the held-out fold, and no amount of
resampling inside the training partition would have revealed that. This is the
strongest argument in the paper for reading the held-out fold at all, and for
counting the readings.

\paragraph{Three further negatives, reported because they were run.} An
anisotropic gate --- ellipsoidal neighbourhoods aligned with the local principal
axes, compressing the surface normal so that score spreads along a patch instead
of into the core, which is the failure mode Section~\ref{sec:results} identifies
as shared with P2Rank --- moves the training pick half by \(+0.0005\) at its
best setting and is not distinguishable from the isotropic gate. Regrouping the
invariants by their measured correlation rather than by what they measure costs
\(0.046\); grouping them to be deliberately uncorrelated costs \(0.059\). The
thematic grouping, chosen for meaning before any of this was measured, is the
better subgroup. Enlarging the bank beyond the six thematic tables per level
degrades monotonically, because the integer fan-out is a rank and a long tail of
mediocre tables collectively outvotes the informative ones.

\paragraph{One implementation note that changed a conclusion.} The first
encoding of the orbit address used a combinatorial number system and was not
injective; distinct orbits collided, the construction measured \(0.6701\) where
it should have measured \(0.7475\), and the idea looked refuted. The two
prototypes disagreed on a quantity that cannot depend on the encoding --- the
partition of residues into cells, and therefore the AUC --- which is what
exposed it. The address in the committed module is the digit word sorted, whose
correctness is a property one can test by exhaustion, and
\texttt{tests/test\_quotient\_tables.py} does exactly that on every case small
enough to enumerate. A wrong encoder here would not have raised; it would have
returned a plausible number.

\paragraph{The capacity attribution does not survive being tested.} A negative
result is only worth the diagnosis it forces. The construction above was built
from this paper's own explanation of why the counting field trails --- that a
dense table cannot address enough digits at once --- and the explanation
predicts that widening the addressable word closes the gap. Four measurements,
all on the \GapPickUnits{}-unit accession-disjoint pick half and none of them a
reading of the held-out fold, say the prediction fails in every direction it can
be pushed. Raising the admissible width from \QuoDenseWidth{} to \QuoSymWidth{}
gains \QuoTrainGain{} on training splits and \QuoDAf{} on the fold. Lowering it
to one digit --- \GapMarginalTables{} marginal tables at \GapMarginalLevels{}
levels, whose \(L \le rN\) bound is unreachable at any resolution used here, and
which can never fall back because no cell is empty --- scores \GapMarginalAuc{}
against the interaction bank's \GapTablesSmall{}. And the bank almost never
falls back in the first place: \GapUnseenPct{}\% of held-out residues address a
cell the fit half never occupied. Capacity is not what binds.

\paragraph{What binds is the fan-out, and \GapExtraWires{} wires.} Holding the
tables fixed and replacing the integer Gini rank with the solved integer fan-out
that the table field uses moves the dense bank from \GapTablesSmall{} to
\GapFanoutSolved{}, worth \GapFanoutPrice{}: most of the same-invariant readout
gap, bought by changing \NAlgTables{} numbers and nothing else. That is the
price of the property this method exists to demonstrate --- no real-valued
coefficient anywhere on the datapath --- and it is now measured rather than
assumed. The quotient bank gets \GapFanoutQuo{} from the same substitution, so
the two devices buy the same statistical good and do not compose; the field
appears to sit against a ceiling near \GapFanoutSolved{} that neither reaches
past. Separately, the fitted linear readout reported in
Table~\ref{tab:metrics} is not the same-invariant comparison and should not be
read as one. It consumes \GapWideWires{} wires --- \NAlgFeatures{} invariants,
seven published residue constants and a training propensity counter, each at
four spatial scales --- against the counting field's \NAlgFeatures{}. On the
pick half the readout is worth \GapReadout{} and the extra wires
\GapInput{}, so \GapInputShare{}\% of the distance between the two published
methods is an input difference wearing a readout's name. The same-invariant
statement, which stands, is the \NAlgFeatures{}-feature Fisher discriminant at
\FisherAll{} against the field's \AlgAuc{}.

\paragraph{Why the training folds could not have predicted the fold.} The
diagnosis also explains the non-transfer, and the explanation is not specific to
this construction. Binning structures by how well the dense bank does on each,
the quotient's gain lives entirely at the bottom: on structures where the dense
bank scores below \GapHardCut{} it gains \GapHardTrainGain{} on the pick half
and \GapHardTestGain{} on the fold, and on structures where the dense bank
already scores above \(0.85\) it loses \GapEasyTrainGain{} and
\GapEasyTestGain{}. The held-out fold holds more of the second kind, but
reweighting it to the training fold's difficulty mix recovers only
\GapReweighted{} of the \QuoTrainGain{} --- composition is the smaller half of
the story. The larger half is that the per-bin gain is itself smaller there. A
structure is hard on the pick half mostly because its cells are thinly counted,
and pooling counts is exactly the remedy; a structure is hard on a fold held out
under the benchmark's own MMseqs2 clustering because it is unfamiliar, and no
amount of pooling training counts makes an unfamiliar fold familiar. Resampling
inside the training partition can manufacture scarcity. It cannot manufacture
novelty. Any device whose benefit is statistical efficiency will therefore be
over-credited by cross-validation on this benchmark, by about
\(\QuoTrainGain{}\) in this instance, and that is a statement about the
evaluation protocol rather than about quotient tables. It is generated by
\texttt{tools/gap\_decomposition.py} into
\texttt{results/architecture\_sweep/GAP\_DECOMPOSITION.json}, and \texttt{make
gap} fails if the manuscript's version of it stops following.

\paragraph{A harder bound sits above the fan-out.} Even the right fan-out cannot
overtake P2Rank on these invariants alone. The Fisher discriminant of the same
\NAlgFeatures{} invariants scores \FisherAll{} on the official fold
(\texttt{results/architecture\_sweep/FEATURE\_CEILING\_DIAGNOSIS.json}), below
P2Rank at the matched point \AlgAucMatchedBase{}. So no \emph{Fisher} readout of
these invariants can close the gap that Section~\ref{sec:results} attributes to
the counting field --- a qualifier this paragraph originally did without, and
Section~\ref{sec:readouts} shows it cannot: on the widened wire set a logistic
fit of the same linear form recovers almost the whole of the difference the
Fisher direction leaves, so what is bounded here is the estimator and not
linearity. A bank of all $\binom{35}{2}=595$ two-digit tables over the
same ranks, fused by the solved integer fan-out, reaches only $0.7725$ on the
training pick half --- matching the continuous linear form of those ranks
($0.7742$), not beating it. The counting field's ceiling under the present
invariant bank is therefore the Fisher discriminant of that bank, and that
ceiling is below P2Rank. Beating P2Rank with a counting construction requires
new invariants, not a better address or a better fan-out of the thirty-five
already in hand. On fourteen training splits the solved fan-out itself is a real
and stable gain --- mean $+0.0126$, worst $+0.0093$, positive on every split ---
but it is a gain toward a ceiling that is still too low.

\subsection{A robust functional was fixed before the fold was read, and it is
still not confirmatory}
\label{sec:prereg}
Table~\ref{tab:metrics} reports the table field ahead of P2Rank by \TabAucD{}
with an interval that contains zero, and Section~\ref{sec:results} draws the
conservative conclusion. A reader is entitled to ask why the mean of the paired
per-unit differences is the functional being summarised at all. It is a fragile
one here, and the case studies say why before any statistics do: the two methods
succeed on different proteins, so the differences are heavy-tailed and their
mean is the least stable summary available. This subsection asks what a better
summary gives, and then --- because the answer is favourable, which is when the
question matters most --- what that answer is and is not worth.

Changing functional after seeing the fold is not available --- the choice would
carry the maximum over however many were considered. So the choice was made on
the training partition. That required something that had never been done:
P2Rank had only ever been scored on the held-out fold, so the one place both
methods had per-unit numbers was the fold whose reading is budgeted. Scoring it
on the \PTwoTrainUnits{} training receptors costs nothing from that budget and
puts it at \PTwoTrainAuc{} there, against \AlgAucMatchedBase{} on the fold; the
held-out receptors are the easier of the two for it.

Both arms have to be out of sample or the exercise is worthless, and the table
field's cells are counted on the training fold. The comparison is therefore on
the pick half of a cluster-disjoint split, with the field compiled on the fit
half alone: \PreRegFieldPick{} against \PreRegPTwoPick{} over \PreRegNPick{}
structures, neither arm having seen one of them. Six candidate functionals were
then subsampled down to the \NUnits{} units the fold presents, at the
pick-half effect and at a half, a quarter and an eighth of it, with calibration
checked by re-running each on sign-flipped differences. The separation is not
subtle. At a quarter effect the mean clears zero \PreRegMeanPowerQuarter{} of
the time and a \(20\%\) trimmed mean \PreRegTrimPowerQuarter{}. Stratifying on
chain length, the obvious label-free covariate, buys exactly nothing:
\PreRegStratPowerQuarter{}, indistinguishable from the mean it was meant to
stabilise.

The \(20\%\) trimmed mean was preregistered and committed in \PreRegCommit{}
before the reading tool was written; \texttt{tools/preregistered\_read.py}
refuses to run unless that artifact is clean in the working tree and its commit
is an ancestor of \texttt{HEAD}, so the ordering is checked rather than
asserted. The forecast was committed with it: at the margin already published
for this comparison the chosen statistic was expected to clear zero
\PreRegForecast{}\% of the time, which is to say the read was predicted to fail.

It did not. On reading \PreRegReadIndex{} of the held-out fold the trimmed mean
gives \PreRegTestDelta{} with interval \PreRegTestCI{} and
\(p = \PreRegTestP{}\). Every robust candidate agrees --- the median at
\PreRegTestMedian{} (\(p = \PreRegTestMedianP{}\)), the win rate at
\PreRegTestWinRate{} above one half (\(p = \PreRegTestWinRateP{}\)) --- and only
the mean and the length-stratified mean fail to resolve. Nothing was re-scored:
the per-unit numbers were frozen by earlier readings and this is a different
summary of them. That is not a new scoring of the fold but it is a new
inferential use of it, so the ledger indexes it as a read.

\paragraph{What the trimmed statistic does not say.} A trimmed mean that clears
zero while the mean does not invites one objection, and the numbers behind it
are reported rather than left to be asked for. The field is ahead on
\PreRegNAhead{} of the \NUnits{} structures and behind on
\PreRegNBehind{}. The \PreRegNTrimmed{} discarded losses average
\PreRegTailLoss{} and the \PreRegNTrimmed{} discarded wins \PreRegTailWin{}: the
tails are asymmetric, and that asymmetry is the whole reason the mean stays at
\TabAucD{} while the bulk sits at \PreRegTestDelta{}. So the honest reading is
two sentences, not one. On the typical structure the field scores better than
P2Rank on this fold. When the field fails it fails harder
than P2Rank does, and the mean, which is the functional that speaks to those
failures, remains unresolved and is reported unresolved beside the trimmed one.
Dropping it once a better-powered statistic had cleared zero would be precisely
the selection this construction exists to prevent. The forecast, which put the
chance of resolving at \PreRegForecast{}\%, is recorded unedited and was wrong
in the favourable direction: it modelled a smaller held-out margin by shifting
the whole distribution down, and what the fold actually shows is a bulk leading
by more than the mean does with a tail dragging the mean back, which a location
shift cannot represent.

\paragraph{And what the preregistration does not license.}
\label{sec:endpoints}
Everything above is true and none of it makes this result confirmatory. The
preregistration fixed a functional before the reading that used it, and the
ordering is checked by commit ancestry rather than asserted. What it did not fix
is the object the functional is applied to. By the time the statistic was
committed the held-out fold had been read \EndReadsBefore{} times,
\EndLineageBefore{} of those by this architecture's own lineage, the most recent
at \EndPriorBestAuc{}, and each of those readings informed the diagnosis that
produced the architecture reported here. That ordering is derived from the commit
graph in \texttt{results/official\_fold/ENDPOINT\_STATUS.json}, which \texttt{make
endpoint} recomputes and refuses to pass if the count no longer holds.

A preregistered statistic over an architecture selected with the fold in the loop
is exploratory evidence, and the correct response is to say so rather than to
lead with it. This paper therefore names the mean paired ROC-AUC as the primary
endpoint --- the summary the comparison was reported under from the first
reading, which needed no preregistration to be the default --- and reports it
unresolved at \TabAucD{} \TabAucDCI{}. The trimmed mean, the median, the
\(10\%\) trimmed mean and the win rate are reported as exploratory:
\EndNExploratoryResolving{} of \EndNExploratory{} of them exclude zero, they
agree with each other, and they are the reason we think a properly locked
external evaluation is worth running. They are not the reason to believe one
already has been.

The distribution they summarise is reported beside them so that a reader need not
take any single functional's word for it. The field is ahead on \EndWins{} of
\NUnits{} chains and behind on \EndLosses{}; the median paired difference is
\EndMedianDelta{}; the worst losses average \EndWorstLosses{} against best wins
of \EndBestWins{}. Winning more often and losing harder is a shape, not a score,
and it is the shape that makes location statistics disagree. A reader who cares
about the typical protein should read the median; one who cares about the worst
case should read the mean; the paper's claim is pitched at the mean because that
is the harder of the two on this evidence.

\paragraph{Where the difference lives, and the answer is nowhere in particular.}
\label{sec:subgroups}
The next question a reader asks is which proteins. A summary over \NUnits{}
chains does not say whether the field wins on the shallow pockets or the deep
ones, on the structures that barely move between apo and holo or the ones that
rearrange. Answering it means cutting the fold up, and cutting the fold up after
the overall comparison came back unresolved is the standard way to manufacture a
result: at \SgNBandTests{} partitions, roughly one exclusion of zero is expected
from noise alone, and every partition here has a story available for it.

Three commitments, all made before the read, are what let the question be asked
anyway. The \SgNCovariates{} covariates are computed by a tool that opens no
prediction, no score and no metric --- apo/holo pocket RMSD taken from
CryptoBench's own pair records, pocket size, positive rate, chain length and mean
B-factor over the labelled residues --- so the groups are definable without
knowing what is inside them, and a test in the suite fails if that tool ever
learns to read a score. The cuts are tertiles by order statistic, so the group
sizes were fixed and recorded before any difference was looked at. And the status
was declared exploratory in the plan, with a rule that no subgroup result may
become a claim, because that is a decision worth making before the numbers rather
than after them.

Nothing survives that a reader should act on. Of \SgNCovariates{} covariates,
\SgNTrends{} show a monotone trend in the paired difference: the Spearman
correlations sit in a narrow band around zero and none has a permutation \(p\)
below \SgBonf{}. \SgNSurviving{} of \SgNBandTests{} bands clears the Bonferroni
level --- \SgBandName{}, \(n=\SgBandN{}\), at \SgBandDelta{} \SgBandCI{} --- and
it clears it by sixteen ten-thousandths while sitting between two thirds that do
not order around it, with its own covariate's trend at \(\rho=\SgBandRho{}\),
\(p=\SgBandRhoP{}\). A band with no dose-response behind it is what a false
positive looks like at this many tests, and \SgNSurvivingWithTrend{} surviving
bands are supported by a trend. We report it and claim nothing from it.

That is a more useful outcome than it sounds. If the difference between the two
detectors were concentrated in the highly cryptic structures, or in the small
pockets, or in the poorly ordered ones, there would be a mechanism to name and a
subpopulation to recommend the method for. There is not. Whatever separates the
two detectors is spread across the kinds of protein this benchmark records, which
rules out the explanations that would have been easiest to offer.

The per-chain distribution behind all of it is Figure~\ref{fig:subgroups}. There
are \SgTied{} ties: on no chain of the fold do the two detectors reach the same
per-residue ROC-AUC. The spread is wide relative to the location --- standard
deviation \SgSd{} against a mean of \TabAucD{} --- with a fifth percentile at
\SgQOhFive{} and a ninety-fifth at \SgQNineFive{}. \SgBigLosses{} chains lose by
more than five ROC-AUC points and \SgBigWins{} win by more. Those counts are the
whole disagreement between the mean and every robust summary of the same vector,
stated as counts so that a reader can pick the summary their application calls
for instead of being handed ours.

\subsection{The F1 margin was a threshold, and we said so before we knew}
\label{sec:matched}
F1 was the one difference this fold resolved, and it compared two different
kinds of object. Our positive call is the top \MatchOurQ{} of residues in each
chain, at a fraction chosen on the training fold. P2Rank's is its own pocket
assignment, a rule its authors fixed and nobody tuned on CryptoBench. A margin
measured that way may be a property of the scores or a property of the
threshold, and the two are not distinguishable from the published number.

Distinguishing them means giving P2Rank what we gave ourselves: the same
per-chain top-\(q\) rule, the same grid from \(0.02\) to \(0.40\), the same
pooled-F1 objective, the same \PTwoTrainUnits{} training receptors, and the
choice made before the fold is consulted. The grid returns \MatchPTwoQ{} --- the
identical fraction ours selected. That coincidence is a fact about the labels
rather than about either method: about nine per cent of a chain's residues bind,
so nine per cent is the calling fraction that maximises F1 for any ranking of
them, and the two matched rules a reviewer might ask for collapse into one.
Tuning is worth \MatchTrainGain{} to P2Rank on those receptors, moving it from
\MatchPTwoTrainNative{} to \MatchPTwoTrainTuned{}.

Set against a published margin of \MatchNativeDelta{}, that gain forecasts
\MatchForecast{} on the held-out fold, which is to say it forecasts a margin
this fold cannot resolve. So the sentence to be written in each case was
committed in \MatchCommit{} before reading: if the matched interval excluded
zero the advantage would be reported as robust to the convention, and if it did
not, the F1 claim would be rewritten as convention-dependent and struck from the
abstract. \texttt{tools/matched\_operating\_point\_read.py} refuses to run
unless that plan is clean in the working tree and its commit is an ancestor of
\texttt{HEAD}.

It did not survive. On reading \MatchReadIndex{} the matched difference is
\MatchBDelta{} with interval \MatchBCI{} at \(p=\MatchBP{}\), P2Rank rising from
\MatchNativePTwo{} to \MatchBPTwo{} while our own F1 does not move at all ---
our published call already \emph{is} the matched rule at this \(q\), and
recomputing it reproduces the committed call on all \MatchNUnits{} structures.
The trimmed mean, declared in advance as the secondary, agrees at \MatchBTrim{}
\MatchBTrimCI{}. Granting P2Rank the best \(q\) the fold itself admits ---
\MatchOracleQ{}, worth \MatchOraclePTwo{} to it, a threshold it could not have
known and an upper bound rather than a score --- leaves us ahead by
\MatchOracleDelta{} \MatchOracleCI{}, which also contains zero. Our own shipped
\(q\) is within \MatchOurOracleGap{} of the best this fold would have given us,
so the convention is not quietly favouring us either.

We therefore withdraw the resolved-advantage claim on F1. What remains true, and
is what Table~\ref{tab:metrics} reports, is that the table field is ahead of
P2Rank on each of ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, MCC and F1 when both detectors are scored at
the operating point each one ships with, and that neither binary difference
resolves once the threshold is held in common. Recall is the exception and
Section~\ref{sec:full} is where it is dealt with: under the deployment rules our
recall is \emph{lower}, and that turns out to be most of the story. This is not
a technicality about statistics: a
practitioner running P2Rank gets pocket assignments, not a top-\(q\) knob, so the
native-call comparison is the one that describes the two systems as they are
delivered, and it stays primary for that reason. It simply cannot be quoted as
evidence about the scores.

One family of results is untouched by \emph{this particular} objection, and it is
the preregistered one. The ROC-AUC comparison of Section~\ref{sec:prereg} admits
no threshold at all: it ranks residues and integrates, so there is no operating
point to match, and \PreRegTestDelta{} \PreRegTestCI{} stands as it did. That the
result which survived rebinarisation is the threshold-free one fixed in advance,
while the one that fell was the threshold-dependent one nobody had preregistered,
is the more useful lesson of the two. It does not make the survivor
confirmatory --- Section~\ref{sec:endpoints} demotes it on a different ground
entirely, which is that the fold had been read \EndReadsBefore{} times before the
statistic was fixed --- but the two objections are independent and it is worth
being clear which one is being answered here.

\subsection{What the margin was made of: a calling budget, not a better verdict}
\label{sec:full}
An F1 difference is a summary of a confusion matrix and it does not say which
part of the matrix moved. Two detectors can reach the same F1 by opposite
routes, one calling few residues and being right about them, the other calling
many and catching more of the positives. Reporting F1 alone leaves that
indistinguishable, and at a matched calling budget it cannot happen at all ---
which is why the budget-matched precision and recall are the numbers that say
whether a ranking is better or merely differently thresholded.

So reading \FullReadIndex{} reports precision, recall, F1 and MCC on four
conventions: each detector as deployed; one common budget; each at the \(q\)
that maximised pooled training F1; and each at the \(q\) that maximised pooled
training MCC. The last of these is not the same convention as the others,
because the two objectives do not agree: on the training fold ours peaks at
\TrainQOurMCC{} under MCC against \FullBudgetQOurs{} under F1, while P2Rank's
stays at \TrainQPTwoMCC{} under both. Every threshold was also re-selected on a
cluster-disjoint half whose residues the scored field had never counted, because
our \(q\) was chosen on the fold that filled our cells while P2Rank's was chosen
on a fold it had never trained on, and an advantage of unknown size is not
something to leave in a comparison the paper leans on. The selection is
unchanged by that move for all four threshold--objective pairs, so here the
asymmetry is worth nothing rather than worth an unknown amount.

Under the deployment rules the two detectors are not doing the same amount of
work. P2Rank calls \FullCallRatio{} times as many residues as our rule does ---
\FullDeployedCalledPTwo{} against \FullDeployedCalledOurs{} over the fold --- and
that buys it recall at the cost of precision. Our precision is higher by
\FullDeployedPrecDelta{} \FullDeployedPrecCI{} and our recall lower by
\FullDeployedRecDelta{} \FullDeployedRecCI{}; both intervals exclude zero and
they point in opposite directions. The published F1 and MCC margins were
summarising that trade. Holding the budget in common removes it: precision
becomes \FullBudgetPrecDelta{} \FullBudgetPrecCI{} and recall
\FullBudgetRecDelta{} \FullBudgetRecCI{}, so at equal cost the same ranking
recovers somewhat more of the cryptic residues at no measurable loss of
precision. That recall interval excludes zero at \(p=\FullBudgetRecP{}\), and it
is reported as an observation rather than a result: recall carried no decision
rule in the plan, the metric was singled out for emphasis only after the numbers
existed, and \(p=\FullBudgetRecP{}\) does not survive a Bonferroni threshold of
\FullBonfAlpha{} over the four metrics of one convention, let alone the
\FullIntervals{} intervals the read computes in total.

Giving each method the threshold its own MCC objective preferred does not change
the verdict either. At \FullTunedMQOurs{} against \FullTunedMQPTwo{} we call
\FullTunedMCalledOurs{} residues to P2Rank's \FullTunedMCalledPTwo{}, recall
rises to a difference of \FullTunedMRecDelta{} \FullTunedMRecCI{}, precision
falls to \FullTunedMPrecDelta{} \FullTunedMPrecCI{}, and F1 and MCC land at
\FullTunedMFOneDelta{} \FullTunedMFOneCI{} and \FullTunedMMCCDelta{}
\FullTunedMMCCCI{} --- both still positive, both still containing zero. The
forecasts written before the read were \FullForecastFOne{} for F1 and
\FullForecastMCC{} for MCC, each obtained by subtracting the training-fold
tuning gain (\TrainGainF{} under F1, \TrainGainM{} under MCC) from the published
margin. Both under-predicted the outcome by roughly half a point of F1, and the
MCC interval was predicted in advance to contain zero, which it does.

The last objection this read answers is about the resampling unit. Every interval
in this paper resamples chains, and chains of homologous proteins are not
independent, so a bootstrap over them can be narrower than the data warrant. The
size of that effect is bounded by the clustering the benchmark already applies:
the fold's \FullGroupsChain{} chains fall into \FullGroupsPdb{} PDB entries and
\FullGroupsClu{} MMseqs2 clusters at ten per cent identity, with no group holding
more than two chains. Resampling entries or clusters instead of chains therefore
changes no interval in this read by more than a factor of
\FullWidestClusterRatio{}. The concern is real in general and quantitatively
absent here, and it is absent because the benchmark's own construction makes it
so rather than because we checked and hoped.

\subsection{Three points are not an axis: the whole threshold range}
\label{sec:curve}
Sections~\ref{sec:matched} and~\ref{sec:full} answer the fairness objection at
three operating points, and three points invite the obvious follow-up question of
whether they were the flattering three. The scores are already frozen, so the
answer costs nothing but a commitment: reading \CurveReadIndex{} rebinarises them
at every calling fraction from \CvLowPct{} to \CvHighPct{} per cent --- \CvN{} cut
points, four metrics, each baseline --- and the plan that fixed it forbids
anything downstream from reading a value off the result. That prohibition is the
only thing separating a curve over \CvN{} cut points from \CvN{} opportunities to
find a favourable one, so it is recorded in the artifact, gated in CI, and the
deployed \(q\) stays where the training fold put it. At \MatchOurQ{} the curve
recomputes a number Section~\ref{sec:full} published and has to reproduce it to
six decimals or the read is void.

Against P2Rank the counting field is ahead at \emph{all} \CvPtwoRFAhead{} of
\CvN{} cut points on F1, with \CvPtwoRFFlips{} sign changes, and the same holds
for precision (\CvPtwoRPAhead{}), recall (\CvPtwoRRAhead{}) and MCC
(\CvPtwoRMAhead{}). The pointwise intervals still contain zero over most of the
range --- F1 excludes zero at \CvPtwoRFExcl{} of the \CvN{} cut points, all of
them at calling fractions well above anything one would deploy --- so this is a
statement about the sign of the difference and not about its significance. It is
nonetheless the statement the fairness objection asked for. Whatever the margin
is worth, it is not an artefact of where the two methods were cut: there is no
threshold in the range at which P2Rank's binary verdicts are better than ours on

\subsection{The cryptic-specific baseline, and what beating it does not mean}
\label{sec:pocketminer}
The comparison so far has one structural weakness that no amount of statistics
repairs: P2Rank finds pockets in general, and this is a paper about cryptic ones.
The benchmark's own paper treats PocketMiner as the representative method for
cryptic-site detection, so a cryptic-pocket paper that compares only against a
general-purpose pocket finder is answering an easier question than the one it
poses. We therefore restored PocketMiner's published checkpoint at a pinned commit
and ran it on the same \PmNUnits{} units.

Two things had to be established before the fold was opened. The first is that
the rebuild is faithful: on PocketMiner's own test set it returns ROC-AUC
\PmSelfAuc{} against the published \PmSelfPublished{}, with its positive and
negative residue counts reproduced exactly, which is what makes the counts rather
than the AUC the load-bearing check --- an AUC can agree by luck and a label
alignment cannot. The second is a caveat, committed in the plan rather than
discovered afterwards. PocketMiner predicts whether a residue participates in a
pocket that opens during molecular dynamics; CryptoBench labels whether a residue
contacts a ligand in a holo structure whose apo form lacks the site. These are
different quantities, and the plan states in advance that a shortfall would be
reported as transfer between two label definitions rather than as evidence about
the task PocketMiner was built for.

On reading \PmReadIndex{} the counting field is ahead of PocketMiner by
\PmAucOurs{} in mean per-unit ROC-AUC, interval \PmAucCIOurs{}, and it survives
the Bonferroni correction over the read's \PmNTests{} tests at
\PmAucBonfOurs{}; PocketMiner sits at \PmLevel{} against our \TabAuc{}, and
the per-chain split is \PmWinOurs{} to \PmLossOurs{} with no ties. The margin is
larger than anything else in this paper and it is the least interesting number in
this section, because P2Rank is ahead of PocketMiner too, by \PmAucPtwoR{}
\PmAucCIPtwoR{}. A gap that both a general-purpose pocket finder and a table of
counted invariants open on the same baseline is a fact about the label transfer,
not a ranking of methods, and it is consistent with what the benchmark's own
authors report. The thresholded picture is the same at every convention: at a
common top-\MatchOurQ{} budget F1 differs by \PmBudgetFDelta{}
\PmBudgetFCI{}, at PocketMiner's own training-tuned budget by
\PmTheirBudgetFDelta{} \PmTheirBudgetFCI{}, and at its trained probability cut by
\PmTheirCutFDelta{} \PmTheirCutFCI{}.

Six CryptoBench test entries appear in PocketMiner's own training, validation or
test data, which can only help it. Removing them leaves \PmCleanN{} units and
moves the difference to \PmCleanAuc{} \PmCleanAucCI{}, so contamination is not
what produced the gap either.

What this licenses the paper to say is narrow, and narrower than the interval
suggests. On CryptoBench's definition of a cryptic binding residue, a
mechanically auditable table of counted invariants ranks residues better than the
published cryptic-site network --- and so does P2Rank. The honest reading is that
this benchmark's labels are further from PocketMiner's training objective than
from either of the other two methods', and that the gap is therefore weak
evidence about method quality and strong evidence that cryptic-site detection and
cryptic-\emph{ligand-site} detection are not the same task. It remains
exploratory on the same ground as everything after
Section~\ref{sec:endpoints}: the fold had been read many times before this plan
was written.

\subsection{The benchmark's own supervised baseline, which is better than ours}
\label{sec:plmnn}
PocketMiner is the cryptic-site method CryptoBench names; pLM-NN is the baseline
CryptoBench itself trains, and it is the harder test. It embeds each residue with
ESM2-3B and passes the embedding through a small trained network, so it is the
comparison that asks whether a table of counted invariants is competitive with a
supervised model that has seen the same training fold. We rebuilt it: the
published weights were read out of the authors' checkpoint without a deep-learning
runtime, and the encoder layer they use was not documented, so it was recovered by
matching their one published worked example across layers --- layer
\PlmLayer{} agrees at cosine \PlmFidelity{}, the next-best layer at
\PlmFidelityNext{}. Running their network on their own published embedding and on
ours agrees to a rank correlation of \PlmRank{}, which is the quantity a ROC-AUC
depends on; the largest absolute probability disagreement is \PlmMaxDp{}, and that
gap is why this is reported as our reproduction of their baseline rather than as a
comparison against a number they published.

On reading \PlmReadIndex{} the counting field is behind. Mean per-unit ROC-AUC is
\PlmLevel{} for pLM-NN against \TabAuc{} for us, a paired difference of
\PlmAucDelta{} with interval \PlmAucCI{}, and the per-chain split is
\PlmWin{} to \PlmLoss{} against us with no ties. The interval excludes zero but
does not survive the Bonferroni correction over the read's \PlmNTests{} tests
(\PlmAucBonf{}), so on the primary functional alone this is a deficit the fold
resolves only uncorrected. The thresholded metrics are less equivocal: at a common
top-\MatchOurQ{} budget F1 differs by \PlmBudgetFDelta{} \PlmBudgetFCI{} and MCC
by \PlmBudgetMDelta{} \PlmBudgetMCI{}, both surviving the correction, and
Section~\ref{sec:curve} finds pLM-NN ahead at all \CvN{} cut points with
pointwise intervals excluding zero at every one of them. There is no operating
point in the range at which the ordering reverses.

It is worth being exact about what this costs the paper and what it does not. The
sentence now in the abstract was written into the plan before the fold was read
under it, precisely so that a losing outcome could not be reworded afterwards, and
it says that the accuracy claim for the counting field is limited to P2Rank and
that the contribution is auditability and model size rather than predictive
performance against a language-model baseline. That remains the correct reading
for \texttt{table\_field}. pLM-NN is ahead of P2Rank as well, by \PlmAucCtxDelta{}
\PlmAucCtxCI{}. Naming detectors: on this fold pLM-NN and \texttt{geometry\_field}
sit at parity, then the counting field and P2Rank within a few thousandths of each
other, then PocketMiner. A reader deciding what to run for raw accuracy alone can
run pLM-NN or the geometry stack; what the counting field still offers is a
different axis entirely: \FairModelOurs{}~MB of integers against a
3-billion-parameter encoder, no floating-point model at inference, and a score
that decomposes exactly into the terms that produced it. Whether that trade is
worth making is a question about deployment constraints, and this paper's job is
to report the trade accurately rather than to argue that it always favours us.

\subsection{All three baselines in one pass, and the metric that disagrees}
\label{sec:grand}
This read is not part of the submitted paper, which evaluates one detector
of ours against the three baselines pairwise. It scores \GrandNMethods{}
methods --- \GrandNOurs{} of ours together with P2Rank 2.5.1, pLM-NN and

\GrandN{} units, so that no cell of the comparison is missing and no two
rows are averaged over different structures. Its result is parity with
pLM-NN on both per-unit metrics, with the two metrics disagreeing about

It is set out in full in \texttt{paper/supplement\_beyond\_submission.tex}

\subsection{A set that has never been read, and what it decided}
\label{sec:external}
Nothing above can settle anything, and the reason is in our own ledger:
\NIndexedReads{} indexed readings of the official test fold across
\NFoldAccesses{} accesses in total, \NArchOnFold{}
architectures of ours scored on those same units, and a \TabAucD{} margin on the
primary functional that is \LeadInSE{} of one standard error. Disclosing that is
necessary and not sufficient. The only repair is a set assembled after the fact,
labelled before any method sees it, and read once.

We built one from the PDB itself. Of \(17{,}332\) X-ray entries released after
\ExtCutoff{} --- the newest release date anywhere in CryptoBench --- at
\(2.5\)~\AA{} or better, \(33{,}725\) protein chains map to \(4{,}858\) UniProt
accessions, of which \(561\) UniRef50 clusters offer an apo chain and a holo
chain together. Externality here is two conditions rather than one. Temporal:
every structure postdates the benchmark, so none could have reached it, its
splits, or a model trained on them. Sequence: no accession and no UniRef50
cluster at \(50\%\) identity is shared with either CryptoBench fold, which cost
\(73\) accessions for appearing in CryptoBench and \(25\) more for having a
relative there. A structure deposited after a cutoff can still be a
near-identical copy of something in the training fold, and a model that learned
the fold from the copy is not being tested on anything new.

Labels come from CryptoBench's own rule, recovered by fitting its training
records rather than reimplemented from its prose: a heavy-atom contact distance,
a treatment of deposited hydrogens, a definition of which residues count as
polymer, and a pocket-motion threshold were fixed by requiring that the rule
reproduce the benchmark's own labelled residues, which it does on \(505\) of
\(510\) records with \(0\) missed. The five it adds are residues absent from the
apo chain, which CryptoBench cannot label and we do not. Because the recovered
pocket-motion figure agrees with the deposited one only to a tolerance, pairs
within a band around the threshold are left unlabelled in either direction rather
than assigned by a number we cannot reproduce exactly. Of \ExtNPairs{} apo--holo
pairs examined, \(161\) are cryptic, \(2{,}264\) are not, and \(247\) fall inside
that band. The result is \ExtN{} units carrying \ExtNPositives{} cryptic
residues, one per cluster --- so resampling chains and resampling clusters are
the same operation here, and the interval below cannot have been narrowed by
relatives entering the same resample.

The order of operations is the whole point, so it is machine-checkable. The set
was written and hashed in one commit. A plan was committed in the next, pinning
that hash, naming the three co-primary comparisons and the Bonferroni level,
fixing the statistic as the mean paired per-unit ROC-AUC already declared primary
for the official fold, pinning the number each comparison was predicted to give,
and writing out the sentence to appear in this paper under each of six outcomes
--- including the three in which we lose. Only then was anything scored. The read
verifies with \texttt{git} that the plan's commit is an ancestor of \textsc{head},
refuses to run if the plan is editable or if the set no longer hashes to what the
plan pinned, and refuses to overwrite its own result without a recorded reason.

the benchmark.

That mean does not name the residues. Applying a rule fixed on one spent unit
(labelled cryptic, counting field at or above the 80th percentile of the chain,
pLM-NN at or below the 50th) to every unit of the spent external set recovers
fourteen residues across nine units and twenty-four mirror residues across eleven
units (\texttt{RESIDUE\_RECOVERIES\_VS\_PLMNN.json}). Residue asymmetry favours
pLM-NN; the inventory is reported because it is the content behind the
twenty-seven / thirty unit split, not because it reverses the mean. Among the
recoveries are residues of RhoA (P61586) and RAC1 (P63000) already carried by the
frozen set. Nothing here is clinical.

A second external question was also answered once. Set~N holds 327 chains whose
every deposited holo partner leaves the site unmoved --- units no published
CryptoBench comparison scores, because every published evaluation unit has a
pocket. Under a plan committed before any Set~N score existed, a unit's score is
the mean of its ten highest residue scores and the statistic is the ROC-AUC that
separates the 57 spent positives from those 327 negatives. The outcome at the
Bonferroni-corrected level is \texttt{none\_resolved}
(\texttt{SETN\_READ.json}): point estimates are 0.5874 for pLM-NN, 0.5457 for
chain length alone, 0.5344 for PocketMiner, 0.5117 for the counting field and
0.4480 for P2Rank, and every corrected comparison of the counting field against a
baseline crosses zero. The question is real; this set is too small to answer it
at the corrected level. Nothing here is clinical.

The PocketMiner margin loses the excuse it had. On the official fold
Section~\ref{sec:pocketminer} declined to read \PmAucOurs{} as a ranking of
methods, because P2Rank was ahead of PocketMiner by \PmAucPtwoR{} --- a similar
amount --- which made the gap evidence about two label definitions instead.
Externally our margin is \ExtAucPm{} \ExtAucCIPm{} while P2Rank's is
\ExtPtwoRMinusPm{} \ExtPtwoRMinusPmCI{}, crossing zero. The two are no longer
similar, which weakens that reading without establishing the alternative at this
sample size.

A set of \ExtN{} units invites the objection that it is simply easier than the
benchmark, which would make the one confirmatory result here cheaper than it
looks. That is checkable two ways and the two disagree, so both are given.

\emph{By label geometry the external set is harder on every axis.} Three
quantities control how hard a per-unit ranking problem is --- how many residues
must be ranked, how sparse the positives are, and how large the pocket is --- and
the third is not a guess. Stratifying the training partition by it
(\texttt{FAILURE\_TAIL.json}) puts the deployed field at \(0.5991\) on units
with fewer than ten cryptic residues against \(0.8766\) above twenty-two, a
spread of \(0.28\) --- larger than every architectural effect in this paper
combined, and the reason pocket size is the axis a difficulty argument has to
be made on. At the median the external units are \ExtDiffLenExt{} residues
long against \ExtDiffLenOff{} for the official fold, carry \ExtDiffCryExt{}
cryptic residues against \ExtDiffCryOff{}, and are \ExtDiffRateExt{}\% positive
against \ExtDiffRateOff{}\%. Longer chains, smaller pockets, sparser labels.

\emph{By achieved accuracy three of four methods score higher on it}, which is
the objection's evidence and it is real. The discriminating case is the fourth.
P2Rank moves \ExtDiffMovedPtwoR{} between the two sets, PocketMiner
\ExtDiffMovedPm{}, the counting field \ExtDiffMovedOurs{} and pLM-NN
\ExtDiffMovedPlm{} --- an ordering that follows how much context each method
reads, from a local surface detector through a structure graph to a
whole-sequence model. \emph{A uniformly easier set lifts the local detector too.}
This one does not, and its chains are \(46\) residues longer at the median, which
is exactly the resource a context-reading method can spend and a local one
cannot.

So the set is not uniformly easier; it rewards context, and every method compared
on it inherits that. What this does not settle is whether the same ordering
appears on a set matched to the official fold for chain length, which is
unmeasured and is the first thing a second external set should control for. The
half of the objection that stands is the sample size: \ExtN{} positive units is
small, and the interval on the confirmatory result is reported with it for that
reason.

Three findings run against us and none is in a footnote. At a matched
top-\ExtQPct{}\% budget the F1 advantage over P2Rank does not resolve externally
either: \ExtBudgetFDelta{} \ExtBudgetFCI{}, with MCC \ExtBudgetMDelta{}
\ExtBudgetMCI{}. As each method deploys, F1 differs by \ExtDeployedFDelta{}
\ExtDeployedFCI{} and MCC by \ExtDeployedMDelta{} \ExtDeployedMCI{}, but that arm
compares two different binarisation rules and Section~\ref{sec:matched} is why we
do not lean on it. So the honest summary is the same one the official fold gave:
the ranking is better and the thresholded verdict is not established.

Next, P2Rank predicts no pocket at all on \ExtAbstain{} unit, \ExtAbstainUnit{}.
With no call made its precision is \(0/0\) and its MCC denominator carries a zero
factor, so both are undefined and that unit silently left both averages ---
removing P2Rank's own worst case from two of P2Rank's own numbers, in our favour
if left unstated. Recall and F1 are defined there and equal zero, so those two
figures keep it. The underlying asymmetry is real and belongs to deployment
rather than to score quality: a fixed per-chain budget always answers, and a
native pocket assignment can decline to.

Last, the ways this set is easier than CryptoBench were written into the plan
before it was read, so that they could not be produced afterwards to explain a
result in either direction. The guard band leaves pairs whose pocket moves
between \(1.5\) and \(2.5\)~\AA{} unlabelled, which removes exactly the
borderline cases; a component counts as a ligand only if CryptoBench accepted it
somewhere, so chemistry new to the PDB since the cutoff is absent; and one unit
per cluster discards a great deal of real data, including a campaign offering
\(534\) apo chains for a single protein. The set is harder in one respect that
matters at least as much: no protein in it has a relative within \(50\%\)
identity anywhere in CryptoBench, so nothing can be carried over from a training
structure of the same fold.

Fairness of the comparison itself rests on facts rather than intent. All four
methods were given one receptor per unit from the same writer that produced the
CryptoBench receptors. P2Rank ran at the version, JVM and configuration recorded
in the official archive, with all \ExtN{} raw output pairs archived under their
digests. pLM-NN ran over the same ESM2-3B encoder, still reproducing the authors'
published example at cosine \PlmFidelity{}; PocketMiner ran from published
weights, byte-identical to the authors' own featurisation on all \ExtN{} units.
Every per-unit ROC-AUC came from one shared function, and all four methods scored
the identical residue universe on every unit: no unit left the intersection and
no unit lost a residue to it, so each paired difference is paired on the same
residues rather than approximately so.

One commitment did the real work and it was made before any of these numbers
existed: no architecture, threshold, feature, quantisation rule or partition bank
changes in response to this read. A method retuned against its own external
validation set does not have one. Nothing changed.

\subsection{A second external read, and what it did not confirm}

Two sets could. Both were assembled from single-particle cryo-EM entries released

and a detector reading sequence does not.

offered as though it did: the primary comparison is between two detectors inside
one modality, where a modality effect largely cancels. What it does bear on is
what a further confirmatory set should be. For a detector that reads coordinates,
it should be X-ray.

\subsection{Taking a score apart, and what the errors turn out to be}
\label{sec:audit}
Auditability has so far been an architectural claim: every table is a look-up, so
in principle any score can be taken apart. In principle is not evidence, and a
reviewer is entitled to ask what taking one apart actually yields. So we took
apart the \AudCases{} case studies of Figure~\ref{fig:cases}, which a committed
rule had already selected and which between them cover a chain both methods
locate, one only we locate, one only P2Rank locates, and one neither locates.

The decomposition is arithmetic rather than attribution. A residue's pre-gate
score is $\sum_k m_k \, \hat p_k[a_k(i)]$ over all \NTabTables{} tables, so the
per-table terms are the score and not a model of it; the gate's neighbourhood
mean is carried as its own term, because spatial smoothing is part of what the
detector does and folding it into geometry would overstate how local the evidence
is. Each table addresses two wires, each wire derives from one of the \(43\)
local quantities, and each quantity belongs to one of the descriptor families of
Appendix~\ref{app:wires}, so the exact terms group into families with nothing
interpolated in between. \AudTablesMixed{} of the \NTabTables{} tables pair quantities
from two different families and have their contribution split evenly, which is a
stated convention and not a measurement: there is no fact about which half of a
pairwise cell belongs to which wire. What is decomposed is each residue's
deviation from its own chain's mean, because tables with large multiplicities
contribute the same amount to every residue in a chain and only the deviation
orders them. The reconstruction agrees with the published per-residue scores to a
relative \AudReproErr{}, which is the warrant for the previous sentences: a
decomposition that did not add up to the scores the fold was scored with would be
a decomposition of something else.

One residue makes the shape of the output concrete. The highest-scoring residue
of \AudEgUnit{} is \AudEgRes{}, ranked \AudEgRank{} of \AudEgOf{}, and the single
largest term in its score is a table reading \AudEgQA{} against \AudEgQB{}: the
residue sits in quartile \AudEgLevA{} of the first and quartile \AudEgLevB{} of
the second, a cell in which \AudEgRate{} of training residues were cryptic
binding residues, or \AudEgTimesBase{} times the fold's base rate, and
\AudEgMult{} of the \NTabTables{} tables happen to be that same pair. That is a
complete statement of one contribution in units a curator can check against a
structure, and there is no step in it that requires trusting the model.

Aggregating over every residue of the four chains rather than the handful worth
displaying gives two findings, and the second is the one we did not expect.
Table~\ref{tab:audit} reports the mean contribution of each coarse family by
residue class, pooled with equal weight per chain because the chains run 96 to 297
residues and residue-weighting would let the largest decide the pattern. The
first finding is that what separates a call from a miss is local geometry, by
\AudGeomMargin{}, while the smoothing term separates them by only
\AudGateMargin{}; and on the labelled residues we miss, the smoothing term
(\AudMissGate{}) supplies more of the score than geometry does (\AudMissGeom{}).
The misses are therefore not residues the field scored wrongly. They are residues
where it had almost no local evidence of its own and scored them only because
their neighbours scored, which locates where the construction runs out.

The second finding concerns the false positives. Residues we call that the labels
do not confirm decompose almost identically to the ones the labels do confirm: no
family separates them by more than \AudGapToFP{}, against \AudGapToMiss{} between
a call and a miss, a factor of \AudGapRatio{}. Whatever those residues are, they
are not the field reading something different. By every family it measures they
are pocket-like, which is what one would expect if an apo structure's labelled
cryptic residues are the subset some holo partner happened to reveal rather than
every residue capable of forming the site. This audit cannot settle that
question, and four chains selected for how the two methods compared on them are
not a fold-level estimate of anything. What it can do is say that the errors are
not arbitrary and point at the residues a curator would have to look at, which is
the practical content of the auditability claim and is not available from a score
alone.

\subsection{\GenTotal{} invariants from two generators, and a ceiling the
counting field declined to collect}
\label{sec:banks}
The invariant bank had been growing by hand, a few descriptors per round, and
the last such round moved the Fisher ceiling by \(+0.0026\). Two generators
replace that habit. The first enumerates \GenOperator{} descriptors along three
axes --- operator family, scale, functional --- over the Gaussian-weighted
adjacency and the combinatorial and normalised Laplacians of the residue
contact graph at five radii, keeping spectral moments, heat-kernel and
resolvent diagnostics, eigenvector localisation at the centre, gyration-tensor
invariants, and the ratios and differences between the smallest and largest
scale. The second adds \GenChain{} more, and exists because of what the first
cannot represent. Both of the first generator's objects --- a graph Laplacian
and a second moment --- are invariant under permuting residues, so no function
of them recovers the chain order, and a positive-definite second moment cannot
distinguish a saddle from a flat patch of the same spread. The second generator
supplies the local Toeplitz symbol of the sequence lags, the shape operator of a
quadric fitted in the frame of the local normal, the non-Archimedean valuation
profile of the single-linkage ultrametric, and the participation and local
shear of the low-frequency modes of the anisotropic network model. Every one is
a closed-form function of residue centroids and chain order: no fitted
parameter, no gradient, no random seed, no ligand.

On \GenNSplits{} cluster-disjoint halvings of the training partition the
combined bank moves the Fisher ceiling from \GenCeilBase{} to \GenCeilFull{},
a lift of \GenCeilLift{}, positive on \GenNPositive{} of \GenNSplits{} splits
with a worst case of \GenCeilWorst{}. The per-family breakdown is worth
recording because it inverts the reasoning the generators were built on. Each
was designed around one family expected to carry it: for the first, the
eigenvector diagonals at the centre, which the hand-built bank had discarded
entirely; for the second, the participation-and-shear pair that separates a
hinge from a lid. Those two are the weakest families in the bank, at
\GenFamDiagonal{} and \GenFamHinge{}. What carries it instead are the trace
functionals at \GenFamTrace{} and the gyration-tensor invariants at
\GenFamGyration{}, both of which are refinements of quantities the hand-built
bank already had. The second generator as a whole, written specifically to hold
information the first provably cannot, adds \(+0.0012\) on top of it: structural
independence and predictive independence are not the same property, and only the
first was established in advance.

The lift is real and it is on the wrong quantity. A Fisher ceiling bounds
\emph{linear} readouts of a bank, and a table is an arbitrary function of the
cell its digits address; the evidence that the distinction bites is already in
Section~\ref{sec:results}, where the \GenWiresBase{}-wire counting field scores
\TabAuc{} on the held-out fold, above the \FisherAll{} Fisher ceiling of the
\NAlgFeatures{} invariants it is built from. So the deciding measurement is what
the generated descriptors are worth to the counting field, and it was made under
the harness that produced the published wire count, on the same seeded
cluster-disjoint halving, the same partition bases, the same integer fan-out and
the same gates, with one thing changed. Carrying each generated descriptor raw
and as its mean, centred difference and local rank over a \(20\)~\AA{}
neighbourhood --- outside every radius used to build it --- gives \GenWires{}
wires against \GenWiresBase{}. Both arms were searched over an identical
six-point ridge grid, widened until each arm's optimum was interior, because the
wider arm needs more shrinkage and the original three-point grid had left it
pinned at the top; the control arm reproduces the published \GenWireControlAuc{}
exactly. The wider bank reaches \GenWireAuc{}, which is \GenWireDelta{}.

The mechanism is legible in the cells. At \GenWiresBase{} wires
\GenWireControlEmpty{}\% of cells are never addressed on the fitting half; at
\GenWires{} wires that is \GenWireEmpty{}\%. The generated descriptors buy
expressive power and pay for it in coverage, and on this fold the exchange is a
small loss. That is the same lesson as
Section~\ref{sec:coverage}, arrived at from the opposite
direction, and it says something the ceiling alone would have hidden: the
counting field's binding constraint on this benchmark is not the expressive
content of its invariant bank. Widening the bank is not the way to a larger
margin, and the held-out fold was not read to establish that.

\subsection{Where the accuracy actually lives}
\label{sec:readouts}
A reader who has met a generalised additive model will ask the question this
chapter has so far avoided: given the same \NTabWires{} wires, would a logistic
regression do as well? If it would, the interesting object is the feature
construction and the table machinery is decoration. The question is answered on
the training partition, on the same seeded cluster-disjoint halving that selected
the published configuration, with every readout given the same wires, the same
quantisation where it quantises at all, and the same gate search. Six readouts
are compared, and each carries its paired bootstrap interval against the
published one over the \RdNPaired{} pick-half chains both scored,
\RdBoot{} draws, resampling chains rather than residues.

The published readout is the highest of the six, and one interval in that last
column contains zero. It is the one that matters. An L2-penalised logistic
regression on the raw wires, converged in \RdNewtonSteps{} Newton steps, reaches
\RdLogitAuc{} against the field's \RdFieldAuc{}: a paired difference of
\RdLogitDelta{} \RdLogitCI{}. On this half of this fold the counting field
cannot be separated from a linear model reading the same quantities. Every other
rung of the ladder \emph{is} separable from it --- the architecture beats its own
simpler variants by margins that clear zero --- but the whole architecture does
not measurably beat a logistic regression. The accuracy is in the wires.

That is worth stating plainly because this chapter could easily have avoided
finding it. The linear control the earlier sections use is a Fisher discriminant,
and on these wires that estimator reaches only \RdRidgeAuc{}, a gap of
\RdRidgeDelta{} that looks like a decisive argument for the tables. It is
instead an argument about the estimator: fitting the same linear form to the
likelihood rather than to a class-separating direction recovers almost all of
the difference. A linear ceiling established with the weaker of two linear fits
is not a ceiling, and the earlier claim that no linear readout of these
invariants can close the gap holds only for the fit that was tried.

The internal steps are separable and worth their own accounting, since they say
what the architecture does buy. Sixteen partitions over one are worth
\RdOneRoundDelta{} \RdOneRoundCI{}, which is the single largest thing the
construction does for itself: one random pairing of the wires
(\RdOneRoundTables{} tables) is \emph{below} the additive model over the same
bins, and only repeated coverage of the pair lattice (\RdNTables{} tables) pulls
it back above. Rounding the fusion to integers is worth \RdUnroundedDelta{}
\RdUnroundedCI{}, so the fan-out cap is a mild regulariser rather than the cost
it might have been --- the deployable form is also the slightly better one.
Quantising into four bins and reading tables costs, rather than buys, relative
to the stronger linear fit. And the spatial mean is worth \RdFieldGain{} to the
published readout, larger than the field's entire margin over the logistic
regression, which places the biggest single term in the construction outside the
table machinery altogether.

None of this revises the published configuration, which was frozen long before
this comparison existed. It does revise what the configuration should be claimed
for. On accuracy the honest statement is that a linear model on these wires is
its equal on this evidence; what the tables provide is the decomposition
Section~\ref{sec:audit} takes apart, an inference path with no floating-point
model in it, and a detector that is a small integer artifact. Those are
properties a logistic regression does not have, and they are the ones the
construction should be argued for.

\subsection{What the method costs, measured rather than asserted}
\label{sec:cost}
Cost is the other question a table-based method invites, and an earlier version of
this chapter answered it with a speed factor of \TabSpeedup{} --- \TabRuntime{}~s
per chain against \PTwoRuntime{}~s --- that was not a measurement. It came from
telemetry taken while the two methods were invoked in
whatever way was convenient: no pinned thread count, no common timing boundary,
and a JVM started afresh for every chain on one side while the other kept its
field in memory. Re-running it under controls reverses the conclusion, so the
factor is withdrawn here rather than defended.

The controlled version fixes the boundary at a receptor file on disk to a score
for every residue in it, asks both sides for one thread, and measures all
\FairChains{} training receptors on one \FairMachine{} held awake on a constant
power source --- an earlier attempt was lost to a machine that slept partway
through, and the tool now refuses to write a file whose repeats did not run under
the same conditions. Labels play no part in a
cost, so this stays on the training fold. Two regimes are separated because they
answer different questions. One process per chain is what a shell loop costs, and
there the counting field is \FairColdRatio{} times cheaper --- \FairColdOurs{}~s
against \FairColdPTwo{}~s --- but \FairJVMPct{}\% of what it is cheaper than is a
JVM reaching its main method, which is a fact about the Java Virtual Machine and
not about pocket detection. One process for all the chains is the steady state a
served deployment actually runs in, and there the ordering flips: \FairWarmOurs{}~s
per chain for the field against \FairWarmPTwo{}~s for P2Rank amortised over the
same set, making \FairWarmWinner{} cheaper by \FairWarmRatio{}.

That reading deserves one more check before it is believed, because asking for a
single thread is not the same as getting one. Measured against wall time, our
process drew \FairParOurs{} CPU seconds per second elapsed and P2Rank's drew
\FairParPTwo{}: Apple's Accelerate framework does not honour the thread variable
for every kernel it dispatches. The uneven grant runs in our favour, so it can
only have flattered the side that already lost. Reading the same comparison in CPU
seconds per chain, which no thread count can tilt, moves it further the same way:
\FairCpuOurs{} against \FairCpuPTwo{}, with \FairCpuWinner{} ahead by
\FairCpuRatio{}. Both readings agree, and the artifact refuses to be emitted if
they ever stop agreeing.

What survives is narrower than a speed claim and is worth stating on its own
terms. Compilation is a single pass of counting over the training fold followed by
one \(K \times K\) solve, taking \TabCompileSeconds{}~s once. The compiled
artifact is \FairModelOurs{}~MB of JSON, \FairModelOursGz{}~MB compressed, against
P2Rank's \FairModelPTwo{}~MB of model, and it is the entire detector: there is no
separate feature extractor to ship, because the quantities are read off
coordinates. Scoring is \NTabTables{} table look-ups and an integer dot product
per residue, with no floating-point model evaluated anywhere, and that step is
indeed nearly free: \FairScoring{}~s for a whole chain. The cost is in reaching
the digits, and feature extraction is \FairFeatPct{}\% of our
\FairWarmOurs{}~s. So the
architecture's own contribution to inference cost is small, and the honest summary
is that this method is cheap to store, cheap to score, and no cheaper than P2Rank
to run.

\subsection{Asked for a pocket instead of a score}
\label{sec:pockets}
Everything above scores residues, and a residue score is not what a medicinal
chemist asks for. The question they ask is where to look, which means candidate
sites, ranked. Our detector had no answer: its pocket entries in the frozen
predictions were placeholders at the origin. So the ninth read built a pocket
stage, and because a construction invented in order to be measured is the easiest
thing in this paper to tune, the construction was committed before the fold was
opened. Take the top-\AlgOperatingPct{}\% residues of a chain, single-link them at
\PkCut{}~\AA{} --- the pinch radius the descriptors already use, not a number
chosen here --- rank the clusters by summed score, and take the score-weighted
centroid. A candidate hits if it lies within a given radius of any labelled
cryptic atom, which is the same target for both methods; it is not the
ligand-based DCA, because the apo structures have no ligand.

This is the one comparison in the paper where the field is ahead of P2Rank by a
margin that survives correction. On the \PkNPaired{} chains where both methods
offer a candidate, the top candidate lands within 4~\AA{} of a labelled site on
\PkOursOneFour{} of chains against P2Rank's \PkTheirsOneFour{}, a paired
difference of \PkDeltaOneFour{} with a 95\% interval of \PkCIOneFour{} and
\PkBonfOneFour{} after correction. The top candidate's distance to the nearest
labelled atom has median \PkOursDist{}~\AA{} against \PkTheirsDist{}~\AA{},
paired difference \PkDistDelta{}~\AA{} with interval \PkDistCI{}. The advantage
is concentrated at the top of the ranking: at three candidates it is
\PkDeltaThreeFour{} with interval \PkCIThreeFour{}, and at larger radii the two
methods converge (\PkOursOneSix{} against \PkTheirsOneSix{} at 6~\AA{}).

Two corrections work against us and are applied rather than noted. P2Rank's
published centre is a cavity centre, sitting in the space a ligand would occupy
and therefore displaced from every residue heavy atom by roughly a pocket radius,
while ours is a centroid of residues; against a target made of residue atoms that
asymmetry flatters us. Scoring P2Rank at the centroid of the residues it assigned
to each pocket instead raises it to \PkCorrTheirsOneFour{} and \PkCorrDist{}~\AA{},
and the difference narrows to \PkCorrDeltaOneFour{} with interval
\PkCorrCIOneFour{}. That arm was noticed after the read rather than planned, so
the verdict is taken from it as the less favourable of the two. And our clustering
yields fewer candidates than P2Rank offers --- \PkOursCandMean{} per chain against
\PkTheirsCandMean{} --- so we are behind on recall as soon as more than one
candidate is allowed: \PkRecallOursThree{} against \PkRecallTheirsThree{} of labelled
residues covered at three candidates. A method that proposes fewer places to look
is easier to be right about first and harder to be complete with.

The result is exploratory twice over. The pocket stage was built for this reading,
and nine indexed reads precede it. What it establishes is narrower than an
accuracy claim and more useful than none: the residue scores carry enough spatial
structure that clustering them recovers a site, which does not follow from a
per-residue ROC-AUC and could not have been read off one.

\subsection{Five sweeps of the construction's own parameters, and what their
agreement means}
\label{sec:saturation}
Everything above varies what the tables \emph{read}. This subsection varies how
they are built, addressed, chosen and combined --- five sweeps, all on
\LadderSplits{} accession-disjoint halvings of the training fold, none reading the
test fold or an external unit, each holding every parameter but one. They were run
to find a lift. None is a lift, and their agreement is the result.

Two conventions are fixed before the numbers. First, each sweep reimplements a
piece of the pipeline rather than editing \texttt{table\_bank.py}, which a code
digest pins over eight files, so each runs the \emph{deployed} configuration
through its own copy and is required to return the frozen numbers: the
quantisation sweep reproduces them to \LadderRepro{} and the truncation sweep to
\TruncRepro{}. Second, one of the sweeps measures the cost of redrawing the bank
at a different seed, \SatReseed{}, and that is the noise floor for any comparison
between two banks. No difference smaller than it is attributed to a mechanism
anywhere below.

\paragraph{The quantisation ladder is at its optimum, and the optimum is a
count.} Every wire becomes a quaternary digit by within-chain rank cut at
quartiles, so its extreme level is the extreme quarter of a chain while cryptic
residues are \AlgOperatingPct{}~\% of the fold at the operating point and fewer
still by base rate. Measured on the training cache, the extreme two per cent of
the strongest wires is about twice as rich as the extreme quartile containing it,
and a cell holding both a rich band and a poor one states one frequency for both.
Moving the cuts to resolve that costs \LadderTailTen{} at ten per cent tails,
\LadderTailFive{} at five and \LadderTailTwo{} at two, positive on
\LadderTailTenSplits{}, \LadderTailFiveSplits{} and \LadderTailTwoSplits{} of
\LadderSplits{} splits. Monotone in how hard the tail is resolved. The reason is
in the same artifact: the median residues behind an addressed cell falls from
\LadderCellsDeployed{} to \LadderCellsTwo{} and the never-addressed fraction rises
from \LadderEmptyDeployed{}~\% to \LadderEmptyTwo{}~\%. Resolution is at best
linear in the enrichment; precision is inverse in the occupancy, and the occupancy
thins multiplicatively across a pair. The deployed quartiles are not an arbitrary
default.

\paragraph{Which wires are paired is seed noise.} All \CompTablesFull{} pairings
come from one seed. Choosing them instead --- sixteen edge-disjoint greedy
maximum-weight matchings on each pair's second-order variance term, fitted per
split on the fit half --- gives \PairInter{} on \PairInterSplits{} of
\LadderSplits{}, against \PairReseed{} on \PairReseedSplits{} for the same
construction at a different seed. Selection buys nothing over chance. It is not
that the criterion is empty: minimising it gives \PairAnti{} on
\PairAntiSplits{} splits, so pairing badly on purpose hurts, and hurts more than a
redraw. Nor is it selection optimism, which was the first hypothesis and is
measurable: the selected bank's mean per-table interaction is
\PairInteractionRatio{} times the random bank's and survives from the half it was
chosen on to the half it was not at a ratio of \PairSurviveSelected{}, against
\PairSurviveRandom{} for the random bank. The chosen interaction is real and it
generalises, and the field is worse for having it. What the matching cannot do is
spread: interaction concentrates on few wires, and a matching may use each wire
once per round, so a round captures only \PairGreedyIdeal{} of the weight an
unconstrained choice of the same size would.

\paragraph{Appended columns are seen by a solve and not collected by the field.}
\CompCols{} integer columns count which of \CompClasses{} chemical classes sits in
which shell, at which walk distance on the contact graph, and in which pairs among
the contacts --- counts over residues of the same receptor, so no alignment runs
and no database is read. Attached by widening the bus they cost \CompWidened{} on
\CompWidenedSplits{} splits; attached by holding the existing pairings and adding
tables over the new columns alone, \CompUnion{} on \CompUnionSplits{}. The
difference between the two attachments is \CompUnionMinusWidened{} on
\CompUnionMinusWidenedSplits{} of \LadderSplits{}, which is smaller than
\SatReseed{} and is therefore reported as a difference and not as a mechanism,
even though widening retains only \CompSurvived{} of \CompTablesFull{} pairings
and the temptation to explain it that way is strong. A ridge discriminant over the
same columns moves \CompFisher{} on \CompFisherSplits{} of \LadderSplits{}. So the
information is present and the field does not take it, and this is the second
column family of that shape: the wire-asymmetry columns of
Section~\ref{sec:banks} behave the same way, and the two families are of different
mathematical character. The two readouts are not generally ordered this way ---
on the deployed wires the field exceeds the solve --- so the asymmetry belongs to
\emph{appended} columns specifically.

\paragraph{Integer rounding is free and the ridge is not doing the work.} The
multiplicities are the rounded solution of one ridge system against the
within-class scatter of the table outputs, and that solve is the only object in
the inference path that is not integer arithmetic. Two of its properties are
reassuring. The cosine between the real solution and its integer rounding is
\GramRound{}, so quantising the multiplicities costs essentially nothing --- the
exactness is not paid for. And the ridge contributes \GramRidgeShare{}~\% of the
diagonal, so the solve reads the scatter rather than a regularised copy of the
class mean difference.

\paragraph{The bank is not compressible, and no table matters.} \GramTraceTop{}~\%
of the trace of that scatter lies in the top one per cent of its directions, about
fifty of \TruncFull{}, and the solved direction sits at cosine \GramCos{} to the
class mean difference. Read as an effective dimension that predicts a bank two
orders of magnitude smaller, and the prediction is false: ranking tables by the
multiplicity the full solve gives them and re-solving on the top \(K'\) costs
\TruncMultFifty{} at fifty-two tables, \TruncMultTwoHundred{} at two hundred and eight and
\TruncMultSixteenHundred{} at one thousand six hundred and sixty-four, smooth in \(K'\) with
no knee. The control decides what that means. Random subsets of the same sizes
cost \TruncRandFifty{}, \TruncRandTwoHundred{} and \TruncRandSixteenHundred{}, which is the ranked
curve, and at two hundred and eight tables random is ahead; ranking by each
table's own variance is worse than random everywhere, at \TruncGiniFifty{},
\TruncGiniTwoHundred{} and \TruncGiniSixteenHundred{}. The tables are interchangeable. Any
\TruncWithinMilli{} of them reach within a thousandth of all of them, and
\TruncWithinMilliRand{} drawn at random do the same. A trace concentration cannot
distinguish information spread thinly over thousands of directions from
redundancy, and here it is the former.

\paragraph{What the five together say.} A saturated exchangeable ensemble is
indifferent to which of its members are chosen, to how they are paired, to whether
a few hundred more are appended, and to how they are individually weighted. Each
of those indifferences was measured separately and each holds. The quantisation is
the one axis on which the construction is not indifferent, because thinning cells
degrades every member at once rather than exchanging members --- and it is at its
optimum. The five are therefore not five failures to find a lift; they are one
statement about what the construction is, and it is the statement that explains
why the appended column families are invisible. At this bank size the marginal
return per table is nil, and it is the count that has been buying accuracy, not
the content of any table.

Two hypotheses were raised in the course of these sweeps and both were refuted by
the same runs that raised them, which is recorded here because a bound argued and
not measured is a correlate. That the conditioning of the solve orders the banks:
the ordering by condition number does not match the ordering by accuracy
(\GramAgrees{}), across \GramSplits{} splits on which every conditioning quantity
varies by less than a part in three hundred. One part of it survives as a fact
rather than an ordering --- selecting for interaction really does cost the bank
independent directions, \GramEffRank{} falling to \GramEffRankSelected{} --- but it
is not what sets the score. And that the bank is effectively fifty-dimensional,
which the truncation curve above refutes directly.

\subsection{The one thing those five left open, and what closed it}
\label{sec:regional}
The five sweeps all vary a \emph{global} choice applied identically to every one
of \CompTablesFull{} tables: one ladder, one pairing draw, one attachment, one
solve, one table count. Their artifacts name what that leaves untried, and the
reason to try it is in Section~\ref{sec:saturation} already --- the solved
direction sits at cosine \GramCos{} to the class mean difference, which is what
one direction fitted to heterogeneous regions looks like. So: let a table
contribute where it is informative instead of everywhere at one weight. Two
experiments were run on that idea, on the same \LadderSplits{} halvings, and
between them they close it.

\paragraph{Regional multiplicities: the heterogeneity is real and it does not
become accuracy.} Chains are routed into \GateRouteRegions{} regions by size, by
polarity, or at random, and each region receives its own damped correction to the
global multiplicities --- \HierArms{} arms over three routers and four damping
strengths, reproducing the deployed numbers to \HierRepro{}. The answer depends
entirely on which baseline the question is asked against, and this is the reason
both are quoted here rather than one.

Against a random per-chain router, the informed routers win: \HierBestVsRandom{}
on \HierBestVsRandomSplits{} of \LadderSplits{} splits. Twelve of twelve is not
noise, and it says the router is reading real structure --- chains of different
size genuinely want different multiplicities. Against the deployed global solve,
the best of the \HierArms{} arms is \HierBestVsDeployed{} on
\HierBestVsDeployedSplits{} of \LadderSplits{}, which is below the \SatReseed{}
reseed floor and is therefore not a lift, and the rest run down to
\HierWorstVsDeployed{}. The price of establishing that was \(\GateRouteRegions{}
\times \CompTablesFull{}\) fitted numbers.

\paragraph{The cheapest correction the construction admits, and it also fails.}
If the signal is regional but the correction is too expensive, the natural move is
a cheaper one. The spatial gate is the only stage in the readout where a
chain-level quantity multiplies the field, so its weight can be routed for
\GateRouteRegions{} numbers rather than \(\GateRouteRegions{} \times
\CompTablesFull{}\). It does not pay either. Against a single global weight
chosen on the same fit half from the same grid --- the comparison that holds
everything but the regional freedom fixed --- routing by chain size gives
\GateRouteSize{} on \GateRouteSizeSplits{} of \LadderSplits{} and by polarity
\GateRoutePolarity{} on \GateRoutePolaritySplits{}. The same two routers beat the
random one, \GateRouteSizeVsRandom{} and \GateRoutePolarityVsRandom{} on
\GateRouteSizeVsRandomSplits{} and \GateRoutePolarityVsRandomSplits{} splits. Both
readings again, and together they say what neither says alone: the constraint is
granularity and not absence of signal. Subdividing costs more than the
subdivision earns, at both prices tried, three orders of magnitude apart.

\paragraph{One comparison in that experiment is not fair, and the direction it
leans is ours.} Every fitted weight also loses to the deployed
\(w=\TabGateWeight{}\), the global one by \GateRouteGlobalVsDeployed{}. That is
not evidence that a declared constant beats a compiled one. The deployed pair was
not declared a priori: radius and weight were chosen together over a small grid by
pick-half ROC-AUC on halvings of this same training fold, and two of those
selections in fact returned \(w=\LinGateWeight{}\). The deployed weight has
therefore seen pick-half information that every arm above is denied, the bias runs
in its favour, and the honest verdict rests on the global-versus-routed comparison
where both sides see the fit half only. Stated here rather than in a footnote
because a reader cannot see it from the numbers.

\subsection{The table topology, which everything above is downstream of}
\label{sec:width}
Seven measurements now say the readout is at a local optimum, and every one of
them varied something a table \emph{does}: how its wires are cut, which wires it
holds, how many tables there are, how they are weighted, whether the weighting
may vary by region. None varied what a table \emph{is}. Every cell in the
deployed bank is a joint state of two quantised wires, so the field is a sum of
pairwise terms and a three-way interaction is not merely unfitted but
inexpressible. That is not a small class of function to be missing. A counting
field is linear in the indicators of its cells and not in the wires, so widening a
table enlarges the function class it can represent rather than only the number of
quantities fitted --- which is the one kind of change none of the seven sweeps
above could make.

Against that, widening thins. A width-\(w\) table holds \(4^w\) cells, so each
unit of width divides the residues behind a cell by four, and the ladder result
above says the construction is not indifferent to that axis.

Three quantities compete for two degrees of freedom --- width, rounds, cell
budget --- so no single arm isolates the width, and both matchings are run. At a
matched cell budget the rounds fall and each wire is covered fewer times; at
matched rounds the budget grows. The deployed arm is the deployed code and returns
the frozen per-split numbers to \WidthRepro{}.

Width two is an interior optimum and both neighbours are worse. Narrowing to
one-wire tables --- a per-wire lookup with no interaction at all, matched on table
count --- costs \WidthOneTables{}, positive on \WidthOneTablesSplits{} of
\LadderSplits{} splits, which is what the pairing is worth. Widening to three
costs \WidthThreeCells{} at a matched cell budget and \WidthThreeRounds{} at
matched rounds, on \WidthThreeCellsSplits{} and \WidthThreeRoundsSplits{} of
\LadderSplits{}. Not one split of twelve, under either matching. Width four costs
\WidthFourCells{}.

The occupancy says why, and it is the same mechanism as the quantisation ladder
seen along a second axis. The median residues behind an addressed cell runs
\WidthOneTablesCells{}, \WidthTwoCells{}, \WidthThreeRoundsCells{},
\WidthFourCellsCells{} for widths one to four --- a clean factor of four per step,
as the arithmetic requires. At width one there are counts to spare and no
interaction to spend them on; at width three the interaction is expressible and
each cell states a frequency from a quarter as much evidence. Two is where those
cross. The construction is not indifferent to its topology, it is at an optimum in
it, and this is the eighth parameter to say so.

One difference inside the width-three pair is worth stating and not explaining.
The matched-cells arm, with fewer tables from fewer rounds, beats the
matched-rounds arm by a margin only just above the \SatReseed{} floor. More rounds
at fixed width means each wire appears in more tables and the tables repeat each
other more, which is the condition under which the fan-out solve is known here to
degrade. That is a plausible mechanism and it is not measured, so it is recorded
as a difference.

\subsection{The one fitted object, and what it turns out to be doing}
\label{sec:decorrelation}
Everything in the inference path is integer arithmetic over table cells except one
step. The multiplicities are the rounded solution of a single ridge system against
the within-class scatter of the table outputs, and that solve is the only
real-valued fitted object the detector keeps. Section~\ref{sec:saturation} reports
two reassuring facts about it --- rounding it costs nothing, and the ridge is a
small share of the diagonal --- but neither says what it is \emph{for}. Given that
the eight sweeps above found no accuracy anywhere else, and that a construction
built from counting has an obvious reason to want its last fitted object gone, it
is worth asking whether a counting rule can replace it.

The question has a sharp form. A rule computed from each table separately can see
that table's own separation and its own variance. The single thing it cannot see
is how two tables covary, so the solve's entire additional content is the
off-diagonal of the scatter. Five arms therefore delete one ingredient at a time,
all rounding onto the same integer range so that inference is unchanged in cost:
the full solve; the same expression with the off-diagonal deleted; a standardised
mean difference; quartile bands, in which a table's weight is fixed by which
quarter of an ordering it falls in and no real number survives; and the sign of
the mean difference alone. A random-sign arm is the control. The deployed arm
returns the frozen numbers to \CombRepro{}.

The off-diagonal is not idle. It is load-bearing by a margin unlike anything else
measured here. Deleting it costs \CombDiag{}, on \CombDiagSplits{} of
\LadderSplits{} splits --- roughly thirty-six times the \SatReseed{} reseed floor,
where every other sweep in this section moves by a few thousandths. The other
three rules land in the same place: \CombStd{}, \CombBands{}, \CombSign{}, none
positive on any split. It does not matter which per-table statistic is used, or
whether the magnitude is kept at all. Sign alone reaches \CombSignAuc{} against
the solve's \CombDeployedAuc{}, which is \CombSignShare{}~\% of the margin over
chance, and random signs reach \CombRandSignAuc{}, so the signs do carry the bulk
of it and the missing third is not recoverable by any weighting of tables one at a
time.

What the direction cosines say is the more interesting half. The solved direction
sits at cosine \CombDiagCos{} to the diagonal solve, \CombStdCos{} to the
standardised difference, \CombBandsCos{} to the bands and \CombSignCos{} to the
signs. It is very nearly orthogonal to every statement a table can make about
itself. Read with \(\cos(m,\Delta\mu)=\GramCos{}\) from
Section~\ref{sec:saturation}, which says the same direction is nearly orthogonal
to the class mean difference, the picture is consistent and it is not the one the
word ``weighting'' suggests: the multiplicities are almost purely a decorrelation.
\CompTablesFull{} tables are drawn from repeated random partitions of
\NTabWires{} wires, so each wire sits in about sixteen tables and the tables restate
each other heavily; what the solve computes is mostly the correction for that
restatement, and only marginally an opinion about which table is informative.

This is the one place in the construction where the fitted object earns its
keep, and it is why the ridge was never cosmetic --- the same repetition that
makes decorrelation valuable is what drove the direction into the null space when
the pool grew without it. It also says what a combinatorial replacement would have
to be. Not a better per-table score: no such score exists, since the target is
nearly orthogonal to all of them. It would have to be an integer decorrelation of
the bank itself, which is a different object from anything tried here and is left
open rather than claimed.

\subsection{The one quantity known to be present and uncollected, and where it is
not hiding}
\label{sec:appended}
Section~\ref{sec:banks} leaves a number on the table and says so. A ridge Fisher
solve gains \BlkFisher{} on \BlkFisherSplits{} of \LadderSplits{} splits from the
\BlkNewColumns{} wire-asymmetry columns under the deployed gate; the counting field
gains \CompWidened{} from the same columns when the bus is widened and
\BlkWTwoSixteen{} on \BlkWTwoSixteenSplits{} when the bank is extended instead. The
information is demonstrably there and the field does not take it. With every readout
axis measured to its optimum, this is the largest identified quantity left, and it
is worth knowing what it is not before guessing what it is.

Two candidate explanations can be dismissed without an experiment. It is not an
interaction the field fails to see, by arithmetic: the solve is linear, so whatever
it extracts is a linear function of the raw columns. And it is not bank dilution,
since the union attachment holds all \CompTablesFull{} deployed pairings and still
reaches only \BlkWTwoSixteen{}.

What remains is variance. A counting field can represent a monotone signal, but it
cannot estimate cell frequencies for free, and the union attachment asks it to
estimate about sixteen thousand of them to carry what the solve carries in
\BlkNewColumns{} coefficients. For a weak signal that is a poor exchange, and it
would produce exactly the observed shape. So the appended block's geometry was
varied over a range of \BlkCellsRatio{}-fold in the number of cells it costs, from
\BlkCellsMin{} to \BlkCellsMax{}, with the \CompTablesFull{} deployed pairings, the
gate, the ridge, the cap and the banding held. The deployed union arm reproduces its
frozen mean, minimum and maximum to \BlkRepro{}.

It is not variance either. Cutting the cell budget by a factor of eight, from
\BlkWTwoSixteenCells{} to \BlkWOneFourCells{}, moves the lift from
\BlkWTwoSixteen{} to \BlkWOneFour{} --- the wrong way. The best arm is
\BlkWTwoFour{} on \BlkWTwoFourSplits{} of \LadderSplits{}, which is
\BlkWTwoFourVsUnion{} against the deployed union attachment on
\BlkWTwoFourVsUnionSplits{} splits: consistent in sign and about a seventh of
\SatReseed{} in size, so it is reported as a difference and not as a lift. Nothing
here closes a meaningful part of the distance to \BlkFisher{}.

The ordering says something the null alone would not. Accuracy is not monotone in
the cell budget at all --- \BlkWTwoSixteenCells{}, \BlkWOneSixteenCells{},
\BlkWOneFourCells{}, \BlkWTwoFourCells{} and \BlkWThreeSixteenCells{} cells give
\BlkWTwoSixteen{}, \BlkWOneSixteen{}, \BlkWOneFour{}, \BlkWTwoFour{} and
\BlkWThreeSixteen{} --- but it is ordered by width. The two width-2 arms are the
best two and the two width-1 arms the worst two, with width 3 between them, which is
the same interior optimum at width 2 that Section~\ref{sec:width} found for the
whole bank. The appended block is not a special case wanting special treatment; it
behaves like any other block of wires.

That is the useful conclusion, and it relocates the gap rather than closing it. The
asymmetry columns carry information that aggregates linearly across many columns,
each contributing little. A solve with one coefficient per column is an efficient
aggregator of exactly that; a bank of quartile cells is not, and no arrangement of
the same cells makes it one. Two possibilities survive, and both are untried. The
banding of these columns specifically: every sweep of the cut points so far moved
all \NTabWires{} wires at once, which thins all \CompTablesFull{} tables' cells, and
resolving only a \BlkNewColumns{}-column block would confine that cost to a small
sub-bank. Or this class of information belongs to a different readout, in which case
the honest statement is that a counting field and a linear solve are good at
different things and the paper already measures both.

\subsection{What a table is allowed to read, and a rule that predicts a null
family before it is measured}
\label{sec:families}
Every subsection above this one varies how the tables are built. This one varies
what they are built from, which is the only axis the others leave untouched, and
it is the axis on which we were wrong.

Six families of columns were appended to the deployed bus and measured under one
protocol: attach by union so all \CompTablesFull{} deployed pairings are held,
hold the gate, the ridge, the fan-out cap and the banding, and report the mean
per-unit ROC-AUC over \LadderSplits{} cluster-disjoint halvings of the training
fold. All six were null. Chemical class of the neighbourhood by shell and walk,
one anisotropy operator swept over radii, fifteen integer invariants of the
\(7\)~\AA{} contact lining, \FamChemCols{} chemical quantities per side chain,
and \GenTotal{} generated descriptors under two generators: the largest of them
is \(+0.0010\), none is separated from a control that adds the same number of
tables over columns already deployed, and one of them is
\(-0.0061\) on \(0\) of \LadderSplits{}. We concluded that the axis was closed,
and printed that conclusion.

Six nulls with no account of why they are null is a habit and not a screen, and
the account, once written down, contradicts the conclusion it was meant to
explain. It is one sentence: \emph{every null family is a function of data the
pipeline already reads.} The detector's input is a residue centroid and a
neighbourhood of centroids. A family that reads only those, however it
transforms them, is a re-encoding, and six measurements say the counting field
does not pay for re-encodings.

The \FamChemCols{}-column chemistry family is the case where this can be checked
by arithmetic rather than argued. The deployed bus already carries seven
constants that are functions of residue type alone --- Kyte--Doolittle
hydropathy, side-chain volume, aromatic ring atoms, formal charge at pH 7.4,
donor count, acceptor count and rotameric dihedral count. Unquantised, that
seven-tuple is \emph{injective on the twenty standard types}, so residue
identity is already determined by wires the detector reads. Quantised into four
bands, which is what a table sees, it resolves \(17\) of \(20\): the surviving
collisions are ALA/GLY, ARG/LYS and ILE/LEU. The chemistry family's whole
contribution is to resolve those three, and resolving them is worth
\FamChem{} on \FamChemSplits{} of \FamChemOf{} splits, behind a control arm at
\FamChemControl{}. Nothing was wrong with the reasoning that proposed it --- a
cryptic pocket opens by side-chain motion, and the eight composition classes
cannot see conformational freedom, since the aliphatic class holds ALA, VAL,
LEU, ILE and MET with rotameric dihedral counts \(0,1,2,2,3\). Every step of
that is true. It was a proposal to resolve a quantiser's collisions, and the
quantiser's collisions are not where the missing accuracy is.

The rule's content is that it is falsifiable in the useful direction: it names
which family should \emph{not} be null. A residue's backbone has four heavy
atoms and a \(\beta\)-carbon whose positions the pipeline reduces to one point
and then discards. Those bytes are on disk, they are not a function of the
centroid, and the conformational state that opens a cryptic pocket is written in
them. Measured under the protocol above, \FamBkbNarrowCols{} such quantities
give \FamBkbNarrow{} on \FamBkbNarrowSplits{} of \FamBkbNarrowOf{}
(\(p=\FamBkbNarrowP{}\), which is not significant), and the same axis taken
seriously --- \FamBkbCols{} columns from \(44\) quantities under three
aggregations --- gives \FamBkb{}, \FamBkbCI{}, positive on \FamBkbSplits{} of
\FamBkbOf{} splits, \(p=\FamBkbP{}\). Its control arm is \FamBkbControl{} on
\FamBkbControlSplits{} of \FamBkbOf{}, crossing zero: the added cells are not
the mechanism.

A family that reads discarded bytes needs a second control, because
\texttt{more\_old} does not catch a family whose columns carry ordinary
per-residue information that happens not to be about the backbone. That control
is a permutation. The same \(44\) quantities are computed, aggregated
identically, and their rows are shuffled within each chain under a fixed seed,
so every column's multiset of values over the chain is unchanged and every
marginal is identical by construction. The only thing destroyed is which residue
each row describes. Permuted, the family is worth \FamBkbPerm{} on
\FamBkbPermSplits{} of \FamBkbPermOf{} --- worse than adding nothing, because
columns of that shape attached to the wrong residue consume cells and carry
noise. The \(+0.0065\) gap between the two arms is the sharpest statement
available that what the field gained is the backbone conformation of the residue
being scored, and not the shape of a column.

The rule has since named a second family and been right about it again. A
residue centroid does not determine \(\chi_1\): a leucine in gauche-plus and one
in trans are different geometry with identical identity, and the deployed bank
carries only per-type constants, which do not move when the rotamer does. Eighty-
seven quantities of side-chain conformation at the same three aggregations ---
\FamSideCols{} columns covering torsions and rotamer wells, completeness of the
deposited side chain, its extension and curl, the directions around it that run
free to a van der Waals wall, hydrogen-bond satisfaction, two chiral centres and
the coupling of \(\chi_1\) to \(\phi\) and \(\psi\) --- give \FamSide{},
\FamSideCI{}, positive on \FamSideSplits{} of \FamSideOf{} splits
(\(p=\FamSideP{}\)) against a control arm at \FamSideControl{} on
\FamSideControlSplits{} of \FamSideOf{}. Permuted under the same rule as the
backbone family, it is worth \FamSidePerm{} on \FamSidePermSplits{} of
\FamSidePermOf{}: the same eight-thousandth swing from best-measured to
worse-than-nothing, produced by shuffling rows and changing no marginal.

A third family passes the same screen by reading something that is not a
function of the scored residue's atom positions at all. Delaunay-tetrahedralise
a chain's heavy atoms, keep the tetrahedra whose circumradius lies in
\([3.0, 6.0]\)~\AA{}, cluster the resulting alpha spheres by single linkage at
\(1.73\)~\AA{}, and the connectivity of the empty space is available as
\(45\) integer and rational quantities per residue: the size and sphere count of
the void it lines, how contiguous that lining is in sequence, its graph distance
to the rim, and its burial measured against the chain's own convex hull rather
than a sphere. None of that survives reducing a residue to its centroid, which
is what the deployed pipeline does. At the same three aggregations,
\FamVoidCols{} columns give \FamVoid{}, \FamVoidCI{}, on \FamVoidSplits{} of
\FamVoidOf{} splits (\(p=\FamVoidP{}\)), against a control at
\FamVoidControl{} on \FamVoidControlSplits{} of \FamVoidOf{} and a permuted arm
at \FamVoidPerm{} on \FamVoidPermSplits{} of \FamVoidPermOf{}. Three families
now, proposed on the same one-sentence screen, measured under the same protocol,
carrying the same two controls, and negative under permutation every time, where
six earlier families were null.

A fourth family reads no coordinates at all, and it is reported here with the
prediction that preceded it because the prediction was wrong. Every deposited
\texttt{ATOM} record carries a temperature factor, an occupancy and an
alternate-location indicator. This pipeline has never read the first; it reads
the second and third only in order to \emph{discard} alternates, keeping the
highest-occupancy copy. All \(770\) training chains carry a varying temperature
factor column and \(332\) carry at least one alternate, over \(2{,}983\)
residues, so the input is present. Because a temperature factor absorbs
resolution, refinement protocol and scaling and therefore does not transfer
between structures, no quantity in the family is a raw value: each is a
within-chain rank, a ratio between two parts of one residue, a deviation from
that chain's own median, or an integer count. \FamDispCols{} columns give
\FamDisp{}, \FamDispCI{}, on \FamDispSplits{} of \FamDispOf{} splits
(\(p=\FamDispP{}\)), against a control at \FamDispControl{} on
\FamDispControlSplits{} of \FamDispOf{} and a permuted arm at \FamDispPerm{} on
\FamDispPermSplits{} of \FamDispPermOf{}. It is the largest single family
measured here.

It was predicted, in writing and before the run, to be the smallest: \(+0.001\)
to \(+0.003\), on the argument that a temperature factor is largely a function of
solvent exposure and that exposure is what the deployed wires already read. That
prediction is off by roughly a factor of three and the step that failed is worth
naming, because it is the same step the screen exists to prevent: reasoning from
\emph{X correlates with Y} to \emph{X is mostly Y}. A temperature factor takes in
crystal packing, loop mobility, partial ordering and the refinement's own
restraint weights, and none of those is a function of where an atom sits relative
to solvent. The screen's record is now four families for four on the sign of the
effect and one for two on its magnitude; it predicts whether a family is live
better than it predicts how live, and that asymmetry is stated rather than
smoothed over.

The prediction also named, in advance, where to look if it failed --- the
alternate-conformer group, which is not a function of exposure --- and splitting
the family at that seam shows that route was wrong too. The
\FamDispBCols{} columns of temperature-factor quantities alone give
\FamDispB{}, \FamDispBCI{}, on \FamDispBSplits{} of \FamDispBOf{} splits, which
is nine tenths of the whole family; the \FamDispAltCols{} columns of alternates
and occupancy give \FamDispAlt{} \FamDispAltCI{} on \FamDispAltSplits{} of
\FamDispAltOf{}, against their own control arm at \FamDispAltControl{} ---
the same value, which is what a family buying only cells looks like when the
separation is exact. The quantity argued to be redundant carries the family by
itself, and the one expected to rescue the argument carries nothing.

One implementation detail is reported because it was checked rather than
assumed. Band membership is a comparison of a circumradius against two
constants, and a circumradius is a ratio whose denominator is six times the
tetrahedron's volume, so a nearly-coplanar tetrahedron carries an amplified
error and can sit anywhere relative to the band. The decision is therefore made
in floating point and re-made in exact integer arithmetic --- deposited
coordinates carry three decimals, so scaling by \(1000\) is lossless and the
numerator and denominator are integers --- whenever the radius comes within
\(10^{-6}\)~\AA{} of an edge. Over the training partition's \(12{,}113{,}689\)
tetrahedra the exact path fires nine times and has never changed a decision,
and it still does not at a margin a thousand times wider. What makes that safe
rather than fortunate is measurable: the nearest any circumradius comes to a
band edge is \(5.57 \times 10^{-8}\)~\AA{}, and the largest error found by
recomputing the flattest tetrahedra in every chain as exact rationals is
\(5.01 \times 10^{-9}\)~\AA{}, a factor of \(11\). The fallback is retained at
one comparison per million tetrahedra because that factor is a property of this
corpus's coordinate precision and not of the construction.

Two lifts of that size measured separately are not evidence that they add. They
are equally consistent with one signal reached by two routes, which is what this
repository found the last time it asked: the generated descriptors of
Section~\ref{sec:banks} were worth \GenCeilLift{} against the Fisher
ceiling of the hand-built bank and \GenWireDelta{} against the
\GenWiresBase{}-wire counting field that actually ships, because that lift had
been the spatial expansion arriving a second way. The two conformation families are a
strong candidate for the same reading --- both are conformation, they are
computed from overlapping atom sets, and a residue in a strained rotamer is
frequently a residue in an irregular backbone --- so the question is settled by
measurement rather than addition. Concatenated into one block of
\FamConfCols{} columns and run on the same \FamAddOf{} halvings against the same
frozen per-split baseline (mean \FamAddNarrow{}), the two families give
\FamConf{}, \FamConfCI{}, positive on \FamConfSplits{} of \FamConfOf{} splits
(\(p=\FamConfP{}\)), against \FamAddSum{} had they been independent. The stack
therefore recovers \FamAddRecovered{} of the additive sum and \FamAddShared{} is
shared, and the combination beats the backbone family alone by \FamAddOverBkb{}
on \FamAddOverBkbSplits{} of \FamAddOf{} and the side-chain family alone by
\FamAddOverSide{} on \FamAddOverSideSplits{} of \FamAddOf{}. Neither family is
redundant given the other, and the shortfall of \FamAddShortfall{} is the part
that was being counted twice. The control arm remains null at
\FamConfControl{} on \FamConfControlSplits{} of \FamConfOf{}.

The pairwise answer does not settle the triple, and the third family is the one
with a reason to behave differently: void is not a function of the scored
residue's own atom positions, where the other two are computed from overlapping
atom sets. All three concatenated --- \FamGeomCols{} columns --- give
\FamGeom{}, \FamGeomCI{}, on \FamGeomSplits{} of \FamGeomOf{} splits
(\(p=\FamGeomP{}\)) against \FamAddSumThree{} if the three were independent, so
the stack recovers \FamAddRecoveredThree{} of the three-way sum against
\FamAddRecovered{} for the pair. The marginal quantity is the informative one:
adding void to the two conformation families is worth \FamAddOverConf{} on
\FamAddOverConfSplits{} of \FamAddOf{}, which is \FamAddVoidKept{} of its
standalone \FamVoid{}. The two families computed from the same atoms share about
a fifth of what they carry; the family computed from the space between them
keeps nearly all of its own. That is what the screen predicted and it is the
first time the screen has predicted a magnitude rather than a sign. The control
arm is null at \FamGeomControl{} on \FamGeomControlSplits{} of \FamGeomOf{}.

Three things bound what this is worth. It is a training-fold measurement and
nothing here is deployed: moving the architecture would make the external read
of Section~\ref{sec:external} a result about a detector that no longer exists.
The two remaining external sets were frozen for that situation and have since been
read under Section~\ref{sec:setbc}, where the stack's advantage over the deployed
detector did not resolve. The lift is
above the \SatReseed{} reseed floor but of the same order, and it is quoted with
its interval for that reason. Against the \texttt{table\_field} deficit the
supervised sequence baseline of Section~\ref{sec:plmnn} still leaves the deployed
detector at \PlmAucDelta{} \PlmAucCI{} on the official fold, and the training-
fold stack's \FamGeom{} is under two fifths of that gap. A later official-fold
probe of the same geometry stack against cached pLM-NN scores reaches parity
(\GeomFieldPlmDelta{} \GeomFieldPlmCI{}); that probe is development evidence on
a fold already read many times, not a transfer proof from the training-fold
lift, and it is not assumed to settle the spent external set. What changed is
not only the standing of a claim about wire families: the wire axis is not
closed, the screen that says which families are null is stated and has predicted
a sign in advance once, and the direction it points at is atom positions rather
than further functions of the contact graph.

\section{Limitations}
The clearest limitation is the one Sections~\ref{sec:plmnn}
and~\ref{sec:external} measure twice for the deployed counting field: 
\texttt{table\_field} is less accurate than the benchmark's own supervised
baseline. On the official fold that deficit is \PlmAucDelta{} \PlmAucCI{} in mean
per-unit ROC-AUC, larger on the thresholded metrics, and present at every calling
fraction we examined; on an external set that could not have shaped this method
it replicates at \ExtAucPlm{} \ExtAucCIPlm{}. A development probe of
\texttt{geometry\_field} on the same official fold reaches parity with pLM-NN
(\GeomFieldPlmDelta{} \GeomFieldPlmCI{}), which is not a resolved win and is not
a confirmatory external read --- and must not be turned into one by re-scoring
the spent Set~A. Nothing in this paper should be read as a claim that either
detector has beaten a protein language model with a trained head on a fresh held-
out set. The claims that survive are an advantage of the counting field over
P2Rank on external data, an exactly decomposable score, and a
\FairModelOurs{}~MB integer artifact --- and the third of those is a deployment
property rather than a scientific one.

The external advantage over P2Rank carries the load that the official fold could
not, and it has limits of its own that are worth stating in one place.
\ExtN{} units is a small set, wide enough that the pLM-NN deficit stops resolving
under the same Bonferroni correction the P2Rank advantage survives; the difference
between those two outcomes is partly power and should not be read as a difference
in kind. The set is easier than CryptoBench in three respects declared before it
was read, of which the guard band is the largest, since it removes precisely the
pairs whose pocket motion sits near the cryptic threshold. Its labels come from a
rule we recovered rather than from code the benchmark's authors released, and
while that rule reproduces their labelled residues with none missed, a systematic
difference between it and their implementation would appear here as a property of
our method. And it is one read: a single external evaluation of a method that has
been developed against one benchmark for the whole of this project is a check that
can pass by chance, not an independent replication. The right reading of
\ExtAucPtwoR{} is that the parity claim has been tested where it could fail and
did not, at this sample size, on this set.

The three baselines are all reproductions, executed locally, and two of them are
reproductions of published models rather than of published predictions. P2Rank
runs from its own release; PocketMiner is restored from the authors' checkpoint and
validated against their published test set; pLM-NN is rebuilt from the authors'
weights at an encoder layer they do not document, recovered by matching their one
worked example to cosine \PlmFidelity{}. The pLM-NN reproduction agrees with that
example on rank to \PlmRank{} but differs in absolute probability by up to
\PlmMaxDp{}, so a fixed-threshold reading of it would be less trustworthy than the
ROC-AUC reading we take. None of these is a comparison against a number the
original authors reported, and a reader who wants that comparison should ask the
respective authors for predictions on this fold. The 38
excluded multi-chain units mean the reported fold is a strict subset of the
official test fold, and the result does not transfer to multi-chain assemblies
without a chain-aware residue index.

The counting field's accuracy is not separable from a logistic regression on the
same wires. Section~\ref{sec:readouts} measures the paired difference at
\RdLogitDelta{} \RdLogitCI{} on \RdNPaired{} training chains, and an interval
that contains zero over a training half is weak evidence of parity rather than
strong evidence of superiority --- but it is the direction of evidence that
matters here, because it is the architecture's own comparison to make and it
does not come out in the architecture's favour. Nothing in this paper should be
read as a claim that arranging these quantities into tables predicts cryptic
sites better than fitting a linear model to them. What the tables are for is
what Sections~\ref{sec:audit} and~\ref{sec:cost} report: an exactly decomposable
score and a small integer artifact. Those are not accuracy claims and are not
offered as substitutes for one.

Two of the four paired intervals between the table field and P2Rank contain
zero, which licenses ``we cannot separate these on this fold'' and not ``these
are equal''. All four point estimates favour the field, and on those two that is
not evidence of superiority: with \NUnits{} units the interval on a mean
difference is roughly \(\pm0.02\) wide, so a margin of \TabAucD{} is the size of
difference this fold cannot resolve \emph{in the mean}. Section~\ref{sec:prereg}
shows that the qualifier is doing real work on ROC-AUC. Under a \(20\%\) trimmed
mean, chosen on the training partition and committed before the fold was read
for it, the same paired differences give \PreRegTestDelta{} \PreRegTestCI{},
which orders the bulk of the fold and deliberately says nothing about its tails,
where the field's failures are worse than P2Rank's and where the mean is the
functional that speaks. But the same section declines to promote that reading,
and the reason is worth restating here rather than leaving in a subsection: the
fold had been read \EndReadsBefore{} times before the statistic was committed,
\EndLineageBefore{} of those by this architecture's lineage. Fixing a functional
in advance does not fix the object it is applied to. The mean is therefore the
primary endpoint and the robust summaries are exploratory, and the limitation
this leaves the paper with is that it has \emph{no} confirmatory accuracy
advantage over P2Rank on any metric. On PR-AUC no ordering is available at all
and none should be inferred from the sign.

Of the two intervals that do exclude zero, neither survives everything we could
think of to do to it. The F1 difference is \TabFOneD{} \TabFOneDCI{} and its
interval clears zero under \TabFOneSeedSig{} of \NSeedProbes{} independent
resampling seeds --- robust to the bootstrap, and not robust to the operating
point: Section~\ref{sec:matched} rebinarises both methods by one rule and the
difference falls to \MatchBDelta{} \MatchBCI{}. Seed robustness and convention
robustness are different properties and only the first had been checked. The MCC
difference is \TabMccD{} \TabMccDCI{} on \TabMccNPaired{} shared structures --- a
lower bound four ten-thousandths clear of zero, which survives \TabMccSeedSig{}
of \NSeedProbes{} seeds. A verdict that changes with the seed is a property of
the draw, so we report MCC as unresolved despite the frozen seed licensing the
opposite, and the counts are in
\texttt{results/official\_fold/SEED\_SENSITIVITY.json}. MCC is a threshold metric
too, and the matched-threshold reading applies to it in principle; we did not
preregister it there and do not report it, rather than adding an analysis the
plan did not name.

The test fold was read \NTestReads{} times over the development of the table
field itself, and that is not the whole story --- the ledger records
\NIndexedReads{} indexed reads and \NFoldAccesses{} accesses across the
programme, \NLineageReads{} of the indexed ones naming this lineage. The
readings were \ReadAucI{},
\ReadAucII{} and \TabAuc{}, with paired differences against P2Rank of
\ReadDeltaI{}, \ReadDeltaII{} and \TabAucD{}. Each change between them was
motivated by a diagnosis made on the training fold --- an unregularised solve
that collapsed as the table pool grew, and a wire set whose ceiling the circuit
had already reached --- and each configuration was re-ranked on the held-out
training half before the fold was touched again. No reading was discarded. Three
further readings followed and are indexed with these three: a counting-field
probe of a different lineage, the preregistered re-summary of
Section~\ref{sec:prereg}, and the matched-threshold rebinarisation of
Section~\ref{sec:matched}. Neither of the last two changed a detector or scored a
receptor again; both are counted because a new summary, or a new threshold, on
frozen per-unit numbers is a new use of the fold. A ledger that counted only
re-runs would make an unlimited supply of such uses free, which is the loophole
it exists to close --- and the one that cost us the F1 claim was of exactly that
kind.

The whole story is larger, and it is the most serious threat to everything
above. Counted from the artifacts rather than from memory,
\texttt{results/official\_fold/TEST\_FOLD\_ACCESS\_LEDGER.json} records
\NArchOnFold{} distinct architectures of ours scored on these same \NUnits{}
units: \NOurDetectors{} that reached the frozen telemetry and
\NStandaloneProbes{} standalone probes, one of which (\texttt{threshold\_field})
lands within four thousandths of the field reported here and appears in no table
in this paper. An earlier revision of this section said the fold had been read
three times, a figure typed from memory of the three probes that produced the
main result. The ledger is generated precisely because that is the sentence in
any paper least worth taking on trust, and it was wrong.

It was wrong a second time, and this is the more instructive failure because the
generator was already in place. The ledger identified an access by two signals:
an artifact carrying a table of per-unit metrics over the official units, or one
declaring a read index. An audit of the field names actually present in the tree
found seven artifacts that satisfied neither and had read the fold anyway.
\texttt{FULL\_EXPANSION.json} is the plainest: its generator loads the test-side
feature cache, takes its labels, and reports twelve ROC-AUCs over
\texttt{n\_test\_units: 192}, storing only the aggregates, so there was no
per-unit table to find and no index was claimed. \texttt{DUAL\_TRACK\_AB.json}
is the sharper one: it \emph{does} carry a table of $192$ per-unit ROC-AUCs, and
escaped because it spells the row identifier \texttt{unit} rather than
\texttt{unit\_id} and the metric \texttt{A\_resolved} rather than anything
containing \texttt{auc} --- and because every one of its \texttt{unit} values is
\texttt{null}, so it scored the held-out fold without recording which units it
scored. The count of standalone probes rises from $13$ to
\NStandaloneProbes{} once a third signal is added, namely that an artifact
reporting a metric while naming the fold's unit count has taken a number off it
whatever shape it stored the number in. The count of distinct \emph{architectures}
is unchanged at \NArchOnFold{}, because all seven are variants already
represented, so the arithmetic below is unaffected; what changed is how much of
the looking was visible. A gate that searches for one spelling of a field is a
gate that measures the spelling, and the six spellings of ``did this touch the
held-out fold'' that this tree contains are now enumerated in one place and
checked together.

The arithmetic this forces should be done by us rather than left to the reader.
The standard error of a mean ROC-AUC over \NUnits{} units on this fold is
\FoldSE{}. The margin over P2Rank is \TabAucD{}, or \LeadInSE{} of one standard
error, and it is the maximum over \NArchOnFold{} architectures. The upward bias
of a maximum over that many correlated estimates is of the same order as the
margin itself, so the ROC-AUC ordering in Table~2 carries no weight and we ask
for none. That is why this paper claims parity in the mean: not modesty, but that
the number is the wrong size to claim with.

The trimmed-mean result is partly insulated from that particular arithmetic,
because its statistic was fixed before the fold was read rather than chosen from
among the summaries the fold makes available. It is not insulated from the
architecture exposure, and that is the exposure the maximum-over-\NArchOnFold{}
arithmetic is about. We mitigate it as far as a held-out fold allows, by
cross-validating the architecture on CryptoBench's own \NSplitsCv{} training
folds and on \NSplitsRh{} redrawn half-splits, where it ranks first every time.
Mitigation is not elimination: \EndReadsBefore{} readings of the held-out fold
precede the preregistration and \EndLineageBefore{} of them are this lineage's,
so the fold was in the loop even though the selection was not run on it. We
therefore report the robust summaries as exploratory and hold the paper to the
mean. None of this speaks to whether any of these margins would survive a
different benchmark, which only a different benchmark can answer.

The absolute values remain modest --- ROC-AUC \TabAuc{} and F1 \TabFOne{} for
the field that ships, and \GrandBestRoc{} for the strongest architecture the
project has built, which is not that one --- so the contribution is the
construction rather than the accuracy. What the construction buys is
auditability and a mechanically
trivial inference path: every input is a closed-form algebraic, chemical or
counting function of the receptor; the compiled state is
\(\NTabCells{}\) pairs of integers plus \NTabTables{} integer multiplicities;
and scoring a residue is \NTabTables{} table reads, an integer sum, and one
spatial average. Whether that is worth anything depends on the application and
not on this measurement. No affinity, design or clinical claim follows from any
number in this paper.

\section{Reproducibility}
Every number above is regenerated from the repository by two commands: the
official-fold runner (fail-closed loader, per-file SHA-256 verification) and the
paired-bootstrap module (10{,}000 resamples, fixed seed). Parallelism across
structures is a scheduling change only and is order-preserving. Optional native
kernels are an operation-for-operation port of the reference implementation and
are covered by a bit-identity test, so enabling them cannot move a reported
value. Frozen artifacts and the telemetry schema are committed alongside the
code.

The environment is measured rather than declared. \texttt{ENVIRONMENT.json}
records the interpreter, the platform, the version of every library the numbers
depend on, the BLAS that NumPy actually bound to, and the P2Rank build together
with the JVM banner behind it; \texttt{requirements.txt} is generated from that
record, and a gate in \texttt{make verify} fails if the two disagree. This is not
bookkeeping for its own sake. An earlier revision of this repository declared a
NumPy pin that segfaults on import under the interpreter used here, so a reader
following the stated environment could not have started, let alone reproduced
anything; and the integer fan-out is obtained by rounding the solution of a
symmetric system, so a coefficient lying near a rounding boundary can land
differently under a different BLAS. The effect is small and bounded, and
recording which BLAS produced a table is cheaper than arguing about it.

The baseline is auditable without a JVM. For each of the \NUnits{} units,
P2Rank's own \texttt{*\_residues.csv} and \texttt{*\_predictions.csv} are
archived beside a \texttt{run.json} carrying the command, the P2Rank version,
the JVM banner and a SHA-256 of each file, all captured at run time. Every
number reported for P2Rank is a transformation of those two files, so a reader
can recompute the strongest competitor in the table from the archive alone. A
gate checks that it is the identity transformation where it should be: the
scores we evaluate must be P2Rank's own \texttt{probability} column and our
positive calls must be its own pocket assignment, unit by unit and value by
value. That gate exists because an earlier harness scored P2Rank from its top
five pocket centres with an invented distance cutoff and a reciprocal-rank
score, measured it at \(0.62\) against a published \(0.81\), and drew a
conclusion from the gap.

\subsection{An independent recomputation, and what it found}
Consistency gates compare artifacts to other artifacts. A mistake made before
the artifacts are written survives all of them, so the repository also carries a
recomputation that shares no code with the harness: it reads the committed
labels and per-residue scores, re-derives the four metrics and the paired
bootstrap from their definitions, and requires agreement to \(10^{-9}\) on 4608
per-unit values across the six residue-level detectors.

Writing it exposed a defect in residue identity. Identifiers were parsed by
taking their trailing digits and discarding the minus sign, so an
expression-tag residue numbered \(-1\) addressed the same slot as residue 1 and
the two exchanged scores: the tag residue inherited a real residue's ranking,
and the real residue -- which may be a labelled cryptic-site residue -- inherited
the tag's. Eleven of the \NUnits{} structures carry such a tag, one of them with
seven colliding pairs. A second copy of the rule, in the P2Rank adapter, failed
oppositely, dropping any row whose label carried an insertion code. Neither
raised; both moved published numbers.

Every detector was rescored on the official fold after the fix. The conclusions
are unchanged: 80 per-unit values moved, the largest by \(0.025\) ROC-AUC on a
single structure on which every method already scores below chance, and no fold
mean moved by more than \(1.6\times10^{-4}\). We report this rather than
silently re-freezing, because the informative part is not that the result
survived. It is that a genuine data-handling error was invisible to every check
that compared artifacts to artifacts and immediate to the one that compared them
to the data, which is an argument about what a reproducibility package should
contain, not about this paper.

Residue identity is now stated rather than implied. A residue is
\((\text{chain}, \text{resseq})\); the insertion code and the alternate-location
indicator are not part of it. That is forced by the label vocabulary --
CryptoBench marks a site with bare integers, so no label can separate 132 from
132A, and a finer key would populate the universe with residues no label could
ever mark positive. An audit records what follows: 47 tag residues across 15
units, five residues sharing a slot in the one structure with insertion codes,
77 units carrying alternate locations, and five labelled residues in four
structures, \(0.15\,\%\) of 3301, for which the apo crystal has no coordinates.
Those five lie outside every method's universe and are therefore neither hits
nor misses, so the recall reported here is over labelled residues \emph{present
in the apo structure}. The count is pinned in the audit, so a data refresh that
begins deleting positives fails rather than shrinking the denominator quietly.
